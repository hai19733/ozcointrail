Binance vs CoinSpot Australia 2026: Fees, PayID, Regulation & Which Fits You?

Last verified: 16 October 2026 (AEST) · By James Walker · Editor, OzCoinTrail

On headline spot trading fees, Binance and CoinSpot can both charge 0.10% — but that comparison hides an important difference. Binance's standard spot fee starts at 0.10%, with eligible users able to receive up to a 25% discount when paying fees with BNB.[1] CoinSpot charges 0.10% on its Markets order book, while its simpler Instant Buy, Sell & Swap service charges 1%.[2]

For Australians, the bigger difference is funding. CoinSpot supports free PayID and direct AUD deposits, while Binance's guidance on direct BSB bank transfer or PayID deposits is conflicting — a January 2026 announcement reported a restoration, while a later-updated help page still lists them as temporarily unavailable.[3]

The two platforms also have different regulatory structures. Both operate within Australia's AUSTRAC AML/CTF framework. CoinSpot additionally holds AFSL 562554, although its licensed financial services relate to non-cash payment products associated with the CoinSpot Mastercard rather than ordinary spot crypto trading.[4]

Reading time: about 7 minutes. The two things people get wrong most often: quoting CoinSpot at 1% and Binance at 0.10% as if they were the same service, and assuming the AUD deposit method in an older guide is live in your own account.

Work out which CoinSpot flow you are actually using — Markets or Instant — then compare the total AUD you pay and the crypto you receive, not the headline percentage.

Fees: they tie at 0.10% — but only if you use CoinSpot's Markets

The most common mistake in this comparison is quoting Binance at 0.10% and CoinSpot at 1%. That compares Binance's trading fee against CoinSpot's Instant Buy fee, which is not the same thing. The fairer picture:

  • Binance spot trading starts at a standard 0.10% maker/taker, with eligible users able to receive up to a 25% discount by paying fees with BNB.
  • CoinSpot Markets (its order-book exchange) is also 0.10% — but it lists far fewer coins and has thinner liquidity than Binance.
  • CoinSpot Instant Buy / Sell / Swap — the default flow on the homepage — is a flat 1%.

So both exchanges advertise a 0.10% headline rate, but they get there differently. On Binance, 0.10% is the standard spot rate for regular users before applicable VIP discounts, BNB discounts or promotional rates. On CoinSpot, 0.10% applies specifically to Markets, while Instant Buy, Sell & Swap charges 1%. That is a real difference for smaller or less-liquid coins.

Compare the quote, not just the fee

CoinSpot's 1% Instant Buy fee does not make the final execution price identical to the Markets order book. Before confirming, compare the final AUD quote and amount of crypto received, rather than looking at the headline fee alone.

Risk: paying CoinSpot's 1% Instant Buy fee while believing you paid 0.1%

The 1% Instant Buy fee is the easiest cost to pay by accident on CoinSpot. It is the flow most new users land on: you enter an AUD amount on the dashboard, the platform shows how much crypto you will receive, and you confirm. Nothing is hidden, but the charge is presented inside a quote-and-confirm screen rather than as a separate order-book fee, which is exactly why it gets lost when people compare platforms by headline percentage.

It happens because CoinSpot runs two different products under one login. Instant Buy, Sell & Swap is a quoted service charged at 1%; Markets is the order book charged at 0.1%. The same A$1,000 buy costs roughly A$10 in fees on Instant and roughly A$1 on Markets. The convenient button is not the cheap one.

To avoid it: open Markets rather than the dashboard's Instant Buy button; check the fee and the final crypto amount on the confirmation screen before you submit; and for a first trade, place a small order and confirm what actually landed in your account before committing a larger one. If you specifically want a coin that only Instant covers, treat the 1% as the price of access rather than a mistake.

If it has already happened

A filled Instant Buy settles at the price you were quoted, so there is no fee to reverse after the fact. If you believe the wrong fee was applied to a completed order, contact CoinSpot support from inside your account with the order reference — and place your next order through Markets so the 0.1% fee applies from the start.

AUD deposits: this is where they split

If you fund your account with Australian dollars, this is the deciding factor for most people. CoinSpot supports free PayID and bank-transfer deposits that typically arrive within minutes, and free AUD withdrawals. Binance's guidance on direct AUD deposits via BSB bank transfer or PayID is conflicting. A January 2026 announcement reported a PayID restoration, while a later-updated help page still lists AUD deposits via bank transfer (BSB / PayID) as temporarily unavailable — so availability has to be confirmed inside your own account, and in practice Australian users may fund through card purchases, P2P trading or crypto transfers instead.

The practical consequence is friction. To use Binance's 0.10% fee, many Australians take a two-step path — buy on a local exchange with PayID, then transfer the crypto to Binance to trade. That adds a transfer fee and a little time. For a one-off A$500 Bitcoin purchase, the additional transfer step introduces both a network fee and extra complexity. Some users may prefer CoinSpot's simpler AUD purchase flow despite its 1% Instant Buy fee, while frequent or larger-volume traders may place more weight on Binance's lower trading fees and deeper liquidity. Coins and liquidity: a mirror image

The two platforms have opposite strengths here. CoinSpot lists several hundred cryptocurrencies and describes itself as offering Australia's largest variety of digital assets — which matters if you're buying smaller altcoins that local rivals don't list. Binance generally offers much deeper liquidity on major pairs such as BTC and ETH, which can mean tighter spreads and lower slippage for larger or repeated trades, even though its Australian coin list is smaller than CoinSpot's.

In other words, CoinSpot is broader, Binance is deeper. If you want to buy a mid-cap altcoin once and hold it, CoinSpot probably has it. If you want to trade BTC, ETH or another major pair repeatedly, Binance's order books will usually give you a better price on each trade.

Regulation and safety: the AFSL detail most people misread

CoinSpot is often described as “the licensed Australian exchange”, and that needs a closer look. CoinSpot does hold AFSL 562554, but this should not be interpreted as an AFSL covering ordinary spot cryptocurrency trading. CoinSpot's April 2026 Financial Services Guide says its licensed financial services are limited to non-cash payment products associated with the CoinSpot Mastercard. Its crypto exchange activities are separately subject to AUSTRAC registration and AML/CTF obligations (CoinSpot has been AUSTRAC-registered since April 2018, one of Australia's longest-running).

Binance, meanwhile, is AUSTRAC-registered but does not currently hold an AFSL, with an application lodged and pending with ASIC. Its former derivatives business was fined A$10 million in March 2026 after more than 85% of Australian clients were misclassified as wholesale — but that concerned derivatives it no longer offers, not its spot exchange.[5]

The upshot: CoinSpot's AFSL is a genuine regulatory edge, but a narrower one than the headline suggests. Both are AUSTRAC-registered for the crypto you actually trade. For a full picture of how the new rules change things, see our crypto regulation guide.

Derivatives: neither offers them

If you want to trade crypto derivatives or use leverage as an Australian retail trader, this comparison ends in a draw — neither platform offers them. Binance's former derivatives arm surrendered its licence in 2023, and CoinSpot has never offered leveraged derivatives. For locally licensed retail derivatives (up to 2x, subject to suitability checks), the relevant option is OKX Australia, which holds a derivatives AFSL.

Binance or CoinSpot: which fits your needs?

Choose CoinSpot if you're buying crypto with Australian dollars to hold, especially smaller altcoins, and you value a free PayID deposit and a simple, local flow. Accept that the default buy is 1% and check the quote before you confirm.

Choose Binance if you're an active spot trader who wants a lower fee and deep liquidity, and you can fund by card, P2P or a crypto transfer rather than a direct PayID deposit. The deposit friction is the price you pay for the better trading conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, Binance or CoinSpot?

It depends on how you trade. Binance charges 0.10% on spot trades, with eligible users able to receive up to a 25% discount by paying fees with BNB. CoinSpot charges 0.10% on its Markets exchange but 1% on the default Instant Buy that most people use. For active trading, Binance is usually cheaper; for a one-off buy, the deposit convenience may matter more than the fee difference.

Does CoinSpot have an AFSL?

CoinSpot holds AFSL 562554, but it covers non-cash payment products associated with the CoinSpot Mastercard — not ordinary spot crypto trading. CoinSpot's crypto exchange is AUSTRAC-registered, the same registration Binance holds. Binance does not currently hold an AFSL, with an application pending with ASIC.

Can I deposit AUD into Binance or CoinSpot?

CoinSpot supports free PayID and bank-transfer AUD deposits and free AUD withdrawals. Binance's guidance on direct AUD deposits via BSB / PayID is conflicting — a January 2026 announcement reported a restoration while a later help page still lists them as temporarily unavailable. Australian users should check their own account and may fund via card, P2P or crypto transfers instead.

Which is better for beginners?

CoinSpot, for most beginners. A free PayID deposit and a simple buy flow are easier to use than Binance's workarounds for AUD funding. Just be aware the default Instant Buy charges 1%.

How do Binance and CoinSpot differ on regulation and security?

Both are AUSTRAC-registered digital currency exchanges under Australia's AML/CTF framework. CoinSpot additionally holds AFSL 562554 (for non-cash payment products only) and states it is ISO 27001 certified for information security. Binance does not currently hold an AFSL (application pending), and in August 2025 AUSTRAC ordered an external audit of Binance Australia's AML/CTF controls after raising concerns about local governance and resourcing. Neither structure is a guarantee — as with any exchange, keep only trading funds on-platform.
