OKX vs CoinSpot Australia 2026: Fees, Safety, AUD Deposits & Derivatives

Last verified: 18 September 2026 · By OzCoinTrail Editorial Team

The short answer: if you're buying or selling crypto with Australian dollars, CoinSpot is the cheaper of the two — its Markets order book charges 0.1%, while OKX charges a 0.70% retail fee on the same AUD pairs. That's the opposite of what most "OKX vs CoinSpot" guides claim, because they quote OKX's non-AUD rates and miss that its AUD pairs sit in a higher fee tier.

But "cheaper" depends on the job. CoinSpot's default Instant Buy costs 1% — more than OKX. And OKX is the only one of the two with licensed derivatives, offering perpetual futures up to 2x leverage for retail clients. So there isn't one winner: these are two platforms built for different things, and the one you want flips depending on whether you're trading spot, using leverage, or just depositing AUD.

The fee most guides get backwards

At the time of writing, OKX Australia charges regular retail users 0.70% maker and 0.70% taker on AUD-quoted spot pairs. CoinSpot's Markets order book charges 0.1%. On a A$1,000 spot trade that's A$7 on OKX versus A$1 on CoinSpot Markets, a seven-fold difference before you even look at the spread.

Why do so many guides say the opposite? The lower 0.08% / 0.10% rates commonly quoted for OKX apply to its Standard Group spot fee schedule rather than AUD-quoted spot pairs — OKX specifically states that AUD fiat-quoted pairs retain their separate fee rules. We flagged the same issue in our OKX Australia review.

Fees change, and OKX runs occasional zero-fee promotions, so treat these as the standard rates at the time of writing rather than a permanent promise — the deposit and trading screens in your own account are always the final word.

CoinSpot's 1% trap — and how to avoid it

CoinSpot has two very different fee structures. Instant Buy, Sell & Swap charges 1%, while trades placed through Markets cost 0.1%. That distinction matters: clicking the convenient Instant Buy option can make CoinSpot more expensive than OKX's 0.70% AUD retail trading fee.

This is the single most important thing to get right on CoinSpot: switch to Markets before you trade. On a A$10,000 buy, 1% versus 0.1% is the difference between A$100 and A$10 in fees. On a genuinely small one-off buy, Instant's convenience is fine — the 1% only really bites as the amount grows.

Neither is "ASIC-licensed" for spot — here's what their licences actually cover

Both exchanges are registered with AUSTRAC as digital currency exchanges, which brings anti-money-laundering obligations. But an AUSTRAC registration is not a financial-services licence, and neither exchange holds an AFSL that covers spot crypto trading.

The AFSL number CoinSpot displays is 562554, held by Casey Block Services Pty Ltd for non-cash payment facilities — it backs the CoinSpot Mastercard and related payment products, not the spot exchange itself. We break down what that licence does and doesn't cover in our Binance vs CoinSpot comparison.

OKX runs three local entities: OKX Australia Pty Ltd (the AUSTRAC-registered exchange, which also acts as distributor for its payment products), OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd (AFSL 379035, which issues the derivatives and margin products), and Bano Pty Ltd (AFSL 536984, which issues the OKX Pay wallet and card). The licensed part — derivatives — is exactly where these two exchanges stop looking alike.

The real difference: OKX does derivatives, CoinSpot doesn't

OKX is one of the few platforms in Australia offering licensed crypto derivatives to retail clients. Retail users can trade USDT-margined perpetual contracts with up to 2x leverage, provided they pass a suitability assessment and a knowledge test, and the products carry negative-balance protection and margin close-out protection. Wholesale clients can access higher leverage, but that's a different category entirely.

CoinSpot offers no derivatives or margin trading — it's a spot-only exchange. That also changes the risk profile: leveraged derivatives can magnify losses, and OKX warns that users may lose all of the USDT deposited in their derivatives trading account.

Getting AUD in and out

CoinSpot offers free AUD deposits via PayID and direct bank deposit. That makes its 0.1% Markets fee especially competitive for a straightforward AUD → crypto trade, because there is no CoinSpot deposit fee stacked on top.

OKX also supports AUD deposits. Local AUD bank transfers currently carry no fee from OKX, although your bank may charge its own transfer fee. Other funding methods, such as card deposits where available, can have different processing fees, which are shown before you confirm the transaction.

AUD bank withdrawals are currently free on both platforms, although external crypto withdrawals are a separate matter and can carry network or transaction fees. If you're planning to move crypto off CoinSpot, see our CoinSpot to Binance transfer guide for what to expect on the way out.

Which one should you pick

The answer flips on what you're actually doing:

  • Plain spot trading with AUD → CoinSpot, but on Markets (0.1%), never the Instant button (1%).
  • Leverage or derivatives → OKX is the only option here, with retail perpetual futures up to 2x.
  • Deepest liquidity at the same 0.10% fee → arguably neither: Binance's spot books are deeper at a 0.10% standard fee, though its AUD bank-transfer deposits are currently unavailable — see our Binance Australia review.

How to actually get CoinSpot's 0.1% (not 1%)

  1. Create a CoinSpot account and complete identity verification (KYC).
  2. Deposit AUD via PayID or Osko — both are free.
  3. Open Markets, not the Instant Buy button on the dashboard.
  4. Find the pair you want (for example BTC/AUD) and place a limit or market order at the 0.1% fee.

Frequently asked questions

Is CoinSpot cheaper than OKX?

For AUD spot trading, yes — CoinSpot Markets is 0.1% versus OKX's 0.70% on AUD pairs. But CoinSpot's default Instant Buy is 1% (more than OKX), and OKX is the only one of the two that offers derivatives.

Does CoinSpot have an ASIC (AFSL) licence?

CoinSpot holds AFSL 562554, but it covers non-cash payment facilities (the CoinSpot Mastercard), not spot trading. Spot trading sits under its AUSTRAC registration. Neither CoinSpot nor OKX holds an AFSL that covers spot crypto.

Can I trade derivatives on CoinSpot or OKX?

OKX offers retail perpetual futures up to 2x leverage, after you pass a suitability and knowledge assessment. CoinSpot offers no derivatives or margin trading.

What is OKX's fee for AUD pairs?

For a regular Australian retail account, OKX currently charges 0.70% maker and 0.70% taker on AUD-quoted spot pairs. The lower 0.08% maker / 0.10% taker rates shown in OKX's newer Standard Group fee schedule apply to other eligible spot pairs; OKX specifically states that AUD fiat-quoted pairs retain their separate fee rules.

Which is better for a beginner?

For a simple buy-and-hold with AUD, CoinSpot is simpler — free PayID deposits and a 0.1% Markets fee once you know to switch off the 1% Instant default. But if you never notice that switch, OKX's flat 0.70% is the more predictable headline.

Is crypto from CoinSpot or OKX taxable?

Yes — crypto is treated as a CGT asset in Australia, so selling, swapping or spending it can trigger a capital gain or loss, on top of any income treatment for things like staking. See our crypto tax guide for the full picture.
