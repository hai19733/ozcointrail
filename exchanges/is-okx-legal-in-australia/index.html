Is OKX Legal in Australia in 2026?

Last verified: 26 September 2026 · Written by James Walker · Source reviewed against OKX, AUSTRAC and ASIC documentation

Yes — OKX is legal to use in Australia, and it is actually one of the more licensed options available. Unlike most global exchanges, OKX runs its Australian business through three local entities, and it holds an Australian financial services licence (AFSL) for its derivatives — something Binance Australia does not. But “legal” still splits by product: spot trading sits under AUSTRAC registration, derivatives sit under an AFSL, and the two carry very different protections. Here is exactly where the line sits.

The short answer

Legal for spot and derivatives — and it holds a real AFSL

OKX is legal to use in Australia and is one of the few crypto-native platforms holding an AFSL. Its spot exchange runs through OKX Australia Pty Ltd, an AUSTRAC-registered digital currency exchange. Its derivatives business runs through OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd (AFSL 379035). Eligible retail users can access limited derivatives up to 2x leverage. The main trade-off is a materially higher spot fee — 0.70% for Australian retail users, versus 0.10% at Binance or CoinSpot Markets.

In short: OKX has two regulators looking at different parts of its business, and the licence coverage is different for each product — which is why a one-word “legal” answer needs unpacking.

AUSTRAC registration is not an AFSL

Most of the confusion about any crypto exchange’s legal status in Australia comes from one misunderstanding: AUSTRAC registration and an AFSL are two different things. OKX is one of the few platforms where both are in play, so it’s the clearest case study of the difference.

AUSTRAC is Australia’s financial-intelligence regulator, and a digital currency exchange registers with it to show it has anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorism-financing controls in place — collecting your ID, monitoring transactions and reporting suspicious activity. OKX Australia’s spot exchange is AUSTRAC-registered for exactly this purpose. That registration primarily relates to the AML/CTF regime; it should not be confused with an Australian Financial Services Licence.

An AFSL, by contrast, is issued by ASIC and authorises specified financial services — including, in OKX’s case, derivatives and margin products. That is a separate licensing regime with its own obligations. So when a comparison site says OKX is “regulated,” the useful question is always: regulated by whom, and for what?

Read that distinction twice

“Registered” answers who is allowed to operate (AUSTRAC, anti-money-laundering). “Licensed” answers what they can sell you and how you’re protected (ASIC, financial services). OKX has both — but they cover different products, and holding an AFSL is not ASIC endorsing the business.

Who runs what: OKX’s three Australian entities

OKX does not run its Australian business through a single company. It splits it across three local entities, each with a different licence or registration. This is the structure most competitor write-ups skip over, and it explains almost everything else about OKX’s legal status.

Spot vs derivatives: two licences, two answers

Whether OKX is “legal” depends on what you want to do on it, because its spot and derivatives businesses sit in two separate legal boxes.

Spot trading is legal and AUSTRAC-registered. Buying and selling crypto runs through OKX Australia Pty Ltd, which is registered with AUSTRAC as a digital currency exchange. That registration is about AML/CTF compliance, not the financial-services consumer protections that attach to an AFSL.

Derivatives are legal and AFSL-licensed. OKX’s leveraged products run through OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd, which holds AFSL 379035. This is the meaningful difference from Binance Australia, whose derivatives business lost its AFSL and stopped serving Australian retail clients. On OKX, derivatives are not only legal — they are actually licensed, and limited access is open to eligible retail users.

This is why blanket claims get OKX wrong in both directions. “OKX is just another AUSTRAC-registered exchange” understates it; “OKX is fully licensed for everything” overstates it. The accurate version: spot is AUSTRAC-registered, derivatives are AFSL-licensed, and retail access to derivatives is capped at 2x.

How OKX’s legal position compares to Binance

Since most Australians weighing up OKX are also looking at Binance, the comparison is worth spelling out — because it flips the usual assumption.

  • AFSL: OKX yes, Binance no. OKX holds AFSL 379035 for derivatives. Binance Australia does not currently hold an AFSL, and its derivatives business surrendered its licence in 2023. For the full picture, see is Binance legal in Australia.
  • Derivatives for retail: OKX yes (up to 2x), Binance no. OKX offers eligible retail users limited leverage; Binance offers Australian retail users no derivatives at all.
  • Spot fee: Binance cheaper. Binance’s spot fee is 0.10%; OKX’s Australian retail spot fee is 0.70%. The licence and the derivatives access are OKX’s edge, not its price.

So the honest summary is not “OKX is more regulated, therefore better.” It’s that OKX is more licensed in one specific area — derivatives — and you pay a higher spot fee for it. What this means for you in practice

Strip the licences away and the practical question is simpler: what can you actually do on OKX as an Australian, and what should you not assume you’re protected against?

You can open an account, verify your identity, buy and sell spot crypto, deposit and withdraw AUD, and — if eligible — trade perpetual swaps and futures up to 2x. You should not assume the spot exchange has the same protections as the derivatives business just because one part of OKX holds an AFSL; the spot side is AUSTRAC-registered, and ordinary spot crypto assets are not automatically treated the same way as regulated financial products.

The fee is the practical catch: OKX’s 0.70% Australian retail spot fee is materially higher than the 0.10% you would pay at Binance or on CoinSpot’s Markets order book. If your goal is simply to buy and hold, that premium buys you the licensed-derivatives access you may never use. We break the fee structure down in the full OKX review. And as with any platform, the standing rule applies: only keep trading funds on-platform, not savings you can’t afford to lose.

Claims that get it wrong

When you read other sites on this topic, four statements crop up that don’t hold up against the current facts.

  • “OKX is only AUSTRAC-registered, like every other exchange.” Understates it. OKX also holds AFSL 379035 for derivatives — a licence most Australian crypto platforms, including Binance, do not hold.
  • “OKX is fully licensed for everything it sells.” Overstates it. The AFSL covers derivatives and payments, not spot; the spot exchange is AUSTRAC-registered, and holding an AFSL is not ASIC endorsing the business.
  • “Australian retail users can’t trade derivatives on OKX.” Wrong. Eligible retail users can access perpetual swaps and futures up to 2x after a suitability and knowledge assessment.
  • “OKX charges 0.08% / 0.10% spot fees in Australia.” Wrong. Those are OKX’s global or wholesale rates; the Australian retail standard tier is 0.70% maker/taker on all pairs except zero-fee pairs.

Frequently asked questions

Is OKX legal in Australia?

Yes. OKX operates legally through three local entities. Spot trading runs through OKX Australia Pty Ltd, which is AUSTRAC-registered as a digital currency exchange, and derivatives run through OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd, which holds AFSL 379035. Retail users can access limited derivatives up to 2x, while spot carries a 0.70% standard fee.

Does OKX have an AFSL in Australia?

Yes. OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd (ABN 14 145 724 509) holds AFSL 379035 for derivatives and margin products. A separate entity, Bano Pty Ltd, holds AFSL 536984 for OKX Pay and the OKX Card. OKX’s spot exchange itself is AUSTRAC-registered rather than AFSL-licensed.

Can I trade derivatives on OKX in Australia?

Yes, with limits. Eligible retail clients can access perpetual swaps and perpetual futures up to 2x leverage, subject to a suitability and knowledge assessment. Wholesale clients can access the full suite up to 100x on some products. Expiry futures, options and spot margin are wholesale-only.

Is OKX more regulated than Binance in Australia?

In one important sense, yes. OKX holds an AFSL for derivatives (379035), while Binance Australia does not currently hold an AFSL and offers no derivatives to Australian retail users. For spot, both are AUSTRAC-registered rather than AFSL-licensed. See how they compare in our exchange comparison.

Why is OKX’s spot fee 0.70% in Australia?

OKX’s Australian retail fee schedule sets a 0.70% maker/taker fee on all pairs except zero-fee pairs. This is materially higher than Binance’s 0.10% spot fee and CoinSpot Markets’ 0.10%, and is the main trade-off for OKX’s licensed derivatives. The 0.08% and 0.10% figures quoted on some sites are OKX’s global or wholesale rates, not the Australian retail tier.
