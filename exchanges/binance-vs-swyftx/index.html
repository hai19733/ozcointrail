Binance vs Swyftx Australia 2026: Fees, AUD Deposits & Which Is Better

Last verified: 16 October 2026 (AEST) · By James Walker · Editor, OzCoinTrail

Binance vs Swyftx: which is better for Australians in 2026? Binance has the lower standard spot trading fee at 0.10%, compared with Swyftx's starting fee of 0.60%.[1] But that doesn't automatically make Binance the cheaper place to buy Bitcoin with Australian dollars.

The difference is how you fund your account and what price you receive when you buy.

Swyftx offers free PayID and bank-transfer AUD deposits, a simpler buying experience and demo trading. Binance offers lower spot fees, deeper global order books and more advanced trading tools. Binance also announced the restoration of Australian bank transfers in January 2026, although some of its support pages still contain older availability warnings.[2]

Quick answer: Choose Binance if you already hold crypto or stablecoins and want lower trading fees. Consider Swyftx if you mainly deposit AUD, buy occasionally and value a straightforward local experience. If you're starting with Australian dollars, compare the final BTC amount quoted by each platform before deciding.

This comparison covers trading fees, spreads, AUD deposits, the cost of buying A$1,000 or A$10,000 of Bitcoin, security, regulation and which exchange suits different Australian users.

Reading time: about 11 minutes. The two things people get wrong most often: treating Binance's 0.10% spot fee as the total cost of buying Bitcoin with AUD, and assuming the reported January 2026 PayID restoration applies to their own Binance account.

Start with the purchase preview on both platforms using the same A$ amount — the fee percentage is the last thing to compare, not the first.

Binance normally charges regular users 0.10% for spot maker and taker trades. Paying eligible spot trading fees with BNB can reduce that rate by 25%, to 0.075%.

Swyftx starts at 0.60%, with lower fees available when a user's rolling 30-day trading volume reaches higher tiers.

How to compare the actual BTC received

If you have A$1,000 available, open the purchase preview on both exchanges at approximately the same time.

Compare:

  1. The total AUD you will spend, including any payment charges.
  2. The BTC quantity you will actually receive after applicable fees.
  3. Any difference between the quoted exchange rate and the wider market price.
  4. Whether the quote is still valid when you confirm it.

For example, if one platform offers 0.01020 BTC and another offers 0.01012 BTC for the same final A$1,000 payment, the first quote delivers more Bitcoin.

Decision: If your goal is to buy and hold BTC, compare how much BTC ends up in your account. If you already own USDT and plan to trade regularly, Binance's low spot trading fee becomes more directly relevant.

Binance vs Swyftx Spreads: What Are You Actually Paying?

A spread is the difference between the price at which an asset can be bought and the price at which it can be sold.

On Binance's spot order book, spreads depend on available buy and sell orders. Liquid markets such as BTC/USDT can have narrow spreads, although this changes continuously.

Swyftx uses quoted buying and selling prices and applies a variable spread separately from its internal trading fee.[4]

Swyftx publishes information about its spreads, so describing every spread as a hidden fee would be inaccurate.

The practical issue is whether the final amount of crypto received is competitive.

Trading fee versus spread

A fee is generally calculated as a specified percentage of the trade. A spread is reflected in the difference between the available buying and selling prices.

That means the spread can vary even when the trading fee remains unchanged.

An A$1,000 BTC purchase and an A$1,000 small-cap altcoin purchase should not be assumed to have identical execution costs.

Spread verdict: Binance's liquid spot order books can be attractive to active traders. Swyftx provides a simpler quoted purchase process. Compare the actual prices rather than treating either platform's published fee as its total cost.

AUD Deposits and Withdrawals: Binance vs Swyftx

For Australians, this may matter more than the standard trading fee.

Swyftx supports AUD deposits through PayID and bank transfer without charging its own deposit fee. Standard AUD withdrawals are also free from Swyftx, although banks may impose separate charges.[5]

Binance's AUD banking availability has changed significantly over time.

Does Binance support PayID in Australia in 2026?

Binance suspended direct AUD banking services in 2023.

In January 2026, Binance Australia's restoration of PayID and bank transfers was reported following a staged rollout, with wider access announced for verified Australian users.

However, some Binance support documents updated after that announcement continued to display warnings saying that BSB/PayID deposits were temporarily unavailable.

Those documents are not fully consistent.

The most practical way to confirm availability is to sign in to your verified Australian Binance account and check whether AUD PayID or bank-transfer deposit and withdrawal methods are displayed and enabled.

Do not assume a payment method is available solely because an older article says it has returned.

Are card deposits more expensive?

Card and third-party payment services can involve different fees and exchange-rate spreads.

Swyftx's current payment documentation distinguishes between Stripe and Banxa. For Australian users, it states there is no card processing surcharge for those channels, but Banxa deposits can carry a spread of up to 2.5%.

That means a zero processing surcharge does not necessarily mean a zero-cost purchase.

Binance displays applicable payment-provider charges and the purchase quote before confirmation.

AUD deposit verdict: If reliable, straightforward AUD funding is your priority, Swyftx is the more predictable starting point. If PayID or bank transfers are available in your verified Binance account, compare the resulting purchase quote before choosing.

Risk: sending crypto between Swyftx and Binance on the wrong network

Splitting your activity across the two platforms — AUD in at Swyftx, trading at Binance — is a legitimate strategy, and it is also where the most expensive mistake in this comparison happens. A withdrawal sent on a network the receiving exchange does not credit, or sent without the destination tag or memo an asset requires, will not simply bounce back to you.

It happens because the network is chosen on the sending side, while the supported deposit networks are set on the receiving side. The same coin can exist on several chains, the deposit address for your account looks similar on each, and an address you saved from an earlier withdrawal may belong to a different network than the one selected today. Chain-level network fees are also charged on top, so the cheapest-looking route is not always the one the receiving exchange accepts.

To reduce the risk: open the deposit page in the receiving exchange first and copy the network and any memo or tag requirement from there, rather than choosing a network on the sending side and hoping it matches; send a small test amount and wait for it to be credited before sending the balance; and confirm the address and network on the confirmation screen each time instead of reusing a saved withdrawal entry.

If it has already gone wrong

Contact the receiving exchange's support immediately with the transaction hash, the asset, the network you used and the sending address. Recovery is not guaranteed and can take a long time where the asset or network is unsupported, so raise it before doing anything else — and do not repeat the transfer.

Binance vs Swyftx Regulation: Which Is Safer in Australia?

Both Binance Australia and Swyftx operate within Australian anti-money-laundering registration requirements. However, AUSTRAC registration is not equivalent to an Australian Financial Services Licence.

It is also important to distinguish a company's licence from the specific service that licence covers.

AUSTRAC registration versus AFSL

AUSTRAC registration concerns anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorism financing obligations.

An Australian Financial Services Licence, or AFSL, authorises particular financial services and products within the scope of that licence.

Holding an AFSL does not automatically mean that every crypto service a company operates is covered by it.

Swyftx's regulatory position

Swyftx Pty Ltd holds AFSL 568543.

However, Swyftx expressly states that its spot cryptocurrency exchange services are not provided under that AFSL.[6]

Certain CFD derivatives available through Swyftx are issued separately by Eightcap Pty Ltd, which holds AFSL 391441.

This distinction matters. It would be misleading to suggest that Swyftx's entire spot exchange has the same protections as a licensed financial product.

Binance Australia's regulatory position

Binance Australia's local entity is registered with AUSTRAC.

Binance Australia has also stated that its AFSL application is pending determination by ASIC. An application is not an approved licence.

Its former Australian derivatives business has faced regulatory enforcement over client classification. This should be considered separately from the availability of its current spot services.

What about account security?

Both platforms provide security features intended to reduce unauthorised account access and custody risks.

Whichever exchange you choose, enable strong authentication, protect your recovery information and understand the risks of holding cryptocurrency with a third-party custodian.

Digital assets held on a crypto exchange are not generally protected by Australia's Financial Claims Scheme.

Safety verdict: Neither Binance nor Swyftx can be declared universally safer simply because one has more features or a particular licence. Examine the service you're using, the entity providing it and your own account-security arrangements.

Tax: Does the ATO Receive Binance and Swyftx Information?

Australian cryptocurrency transactions can have tax consequences, including capital gains tax events when cryptocurrency is sold or exchanged.

The ATO operates crypto asset data-matching arrangements and may obtain information from service providers.

Do not assume transactions on a global exchange are invisible to Australian tax authorities.

Keep records of purchase amounts, transaction dates, disposal values, trading fees, transfers between wallets and other relevant transactions.

A transfer between wallets you own is different from selling one cryptocurrency for another, although transaction costs and other circumstances may still affect your tax records.

Tax verdict: Choose a platform based on suitability, cost and supported services — not on an assumption that using one exchange avoids Australian tax reporting.

Binance vs Swyftx: Which Should You Choose?

The right exchange depends on whether you're mainly moving Australian dollars into crypto or actively trading cryptocurrency you already hold.

Choose Binance if:

  • You already hold USDT or another supported trading asset.
  • You trade frequently and want lower standard spot fees.
  • You want a more advanced order-book trading interface.
  • You prioritise access to liquid global crypto markets.
  • You're comfortable checking trading pairs, order types and regional restrictions.

Choose Swyftx if:

  • You're making your first cryptocurrency purchase with AUD.
  • You want free PayID or bank-transfer AUD deposits.
  • You value a simpler interface and demo trading.
  • You need SMSF, company or trust account options.
  • You prefer a platform focused on the Australian customer experience.

Consider comparing both if:

  • You buy Bitcoin regularly with Australian dollars.
  • You're unsure how much a funding conversion costs.
  • You want to know whether the lower Binance spot fee offsets Swyftx's quoted buy price.
  • You're placing larger orders where execution prices matter.

Our final verdict

Binance wins on published standard spot trading fees. Swyftx wins on simplicity and clearly documented Australian-dollar funding. Neither wins every comparison of actual buying cost.

If you're already holding USDT and trading on a liquid spot market, Binance's 0.10% fee — or 0.075% when the BNB discount applies — can offer a significant advantage.

If you're starting with A$1,000 in your bank account, the better decision is to compare both platforms' actual BTC purchase quotes, including payment charges and conversions.

Ultimately, the question isn't which exchange advertises the smallest percentage. It's which exchange delivers the right service and price for the transaction you intend to make.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Binance or Swyftx cheaper in Australia in 2026?

Binance has the lower standard spot trading fee at 0.10%, compared with Swyftx's starting rate of 0.60%. However, a purchase funded with AUD may involve different conversion costs, spreads or payment-provider charges. Compare the final BTC quantity received for the same total AUD payment.

Can I deposit AUD into Binance using PayID?

Binance Australia announced the restoration of PayID and bank transfers in January 2026. However, some official help pages have continued to show older warnings about unavailable deposits. Check your verified Binance account for currently enabled AUD funding methods before transferring money.

Does Swyftx charge a spread on top of its trading fee?

Yes. Swyftx's quoted buy and sell prices can include a variable spread, while the standard internal trading fee begins at 0.60%. The actual execution price and resulting amount of cryptocurrency are therefore important when comparing total costs.

Is Swyftx better for Australian beginners?

Swyftx is often easier for beginners because it offers familiar AUD payment methods, demo trading and a simpler purchase process. Binance may suit users who are comfortable with spot order books and want lower trading fees.

Does Swyftx have an AFSL?

Yes. Swyftx Pty Ltd holds AFSL 568543, but Swyftx states that its spot cryptocurrency exchange services are not provided under that licence. Certain derivative products distributed through Swyftx are issued by Eightcap under a separate AFSL.

Can Australians trade Binance futures?

Binance's Australian offering is focused on spot trading, and global futures products should not be assumed to be available to Australian retail customers. Check current regional eligibility before making any derivatives-related plans.

Which exchange is better for buying A$1,000 of Bitcoin?

The cheapest exchange cannot be established from trading fees alone. Compare purchase previews from both platforms using the same A$1,000 total outlay. The one delivering more BTC under comparable conditions has the better effective purchase price at that moment.

Is crypto held on Binance or Swyftx government guaranteed?

No. Cryptocurrency holdings on these exchanges are not equivalent to protected deposits at an Australian bank. They remain exposed to market, custody and operational risks.

Can I use Swyftx and Binance together?

Yes, subject to account eligibility. Some Australian users may prefer one platform for AUD funding and another for trading. However, transferring cryptocurrency between exchanges introduces network fees, transfer delays and the risk of using the wrong network or destination details.
