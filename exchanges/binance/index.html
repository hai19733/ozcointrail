Binance Australia Review 2026: Fees, AUD Deposits & Regulation

Last verified: 16 October 2026 (AEST) · By James Walker · Editor, OzCoinTrail

Binance has one of the lowest standard spot trading fees available to Australians, at 0.10% maker/taker with a 25% BNB fee discount reducing it to 0.075%, and the deepest order books of any exchange open to Australians. The trade-off is on the Australian dollar side: Binance announced the restoration of direct PayID and bank-transfer AUD deposits in January 2026, though some of its support pages still show older availability warnings, and it does not currently hold an AFSL. This review sets out exactly what that means for you, so you can decide whether Binance fits how you actually trade.

Reading time: about 9 minutes. The two things people get wrong most often: reusing saved PayID or BSB details from an earlier transfer, and treating the 0.10% spot fee as the whole cost when a card purchase or P2P trade is what actually funds the account.

Start by opening the deposit screen inside your own Binance account and confirming which AUD methods it actually shows — then work out the all-in cost of that route before you send anything.

Fees explained

Binance's headline number is simple: a flat 0.10% maker and taker fee on standard spot pairs. Turn on “Use BNB to pay for fees” in your account settings and that drops 25% to 0.075%.[1] At the volumes most retail traders actually hit, this is among the lowest standard spot fees offered by major exchanges commonly used in Australia — but note the fine print: the discount means holding BNB, and BNB's own price moves while you hold it.

Where Binance can get expensive is getting money in. Card purchases may include transaction or payment-provider charges shown at checkout, which can outweigh the 0.10% spot-fee advantage on smaller purchases. P2P pricing also depends on the merchant or counterparty quote, so compare the effective AUD-to-crypto rate rather than looking only at the headline trading fee.

AUD deposits & withdrawals: the PayID situation

Binance announced the restoration of direct AUD deposits and withdrawals via PayID and standard bank transfer (BSB/account number) in January 2026, following a roughly two-year freeze that began in mid-2023 when its local banking partner withdrew support. The reported return was rolled out in stages to verified customers, and Binance has published zero-fee deposits for these methods. However, some official support pages updated after the announcement still show BSB/PayID deposits as temporarily unavailable, so the documentation is not fully consistent.[2] Card purchases and P2P trading remain available as alternatives, but a PayID or bank-transfer deposit is usually the lower-cost route.

Availability can vary

The January 2026 restoration was announced to verified customers, but Binance's own support documentation has not been fully consistent — some help pages updated after the announcement still show BSB/PayID deposits as temporarily unavailable. Check which AUD deposit methods appear in your own account before funding, and review any deposit limits or verification requirements shown at the time.

If PayID deposits do not appear in your account, a card purchase or P2P trade is the alternative, though both can carry higher costs than a direct bank transfer. For a step-by-step on checking whether PayID is available in your account and comparing the routes, see our guide on Binance Australia PayID deposits.

What you can trade

Binance offers spot trading, a P2P marketplace and Earn products to Australian users. The spot market is the core strength: deep liquidity across hundreds of coins means tighter spreads and less slippage than smaller local exchanges, especially outside the biggest pairs.

What you cannot do in Australia is trade derivatives. Binance's former derivatives business (operated through Oztures Trading Pty Ltd) surrendered its Australian financial services licence in 2023, and on 27 March 2026 the Federal Court ordered it to pay a A$10 million penalty after more than 85% of Australian clients were misclassified as wholesale over a nine-month period, exposing 524 retail clients to high-risk derivatives.[3] Spot trading was not the subject of that action and remains available; derivatives are not.

Regulation & safety

Binance Australia is registered with AUSTRAC as a Virtual Asset Service Provider.[4] It does not currently hold an AFSL (the ASIC licence that authorises specified financial services), although Binance says an AFSL application has been lodged with ASIC and remains pending determination. That distinction matters more in 2026 than it used to: Australia's new digital-asset framework received Royal Assent on 8 April 2026 and its main provisions commence on 9 April 2027, bringing digital asset platforms into a new licensing regime, and expanded Travel Rule obligations for covered providers started on 1 July 2026.[5] Read our crypto regulation guide to understand where Binance sits under the new rules.

On operational security, Binance has the resources of the world's largest exchange behind it — including large asset reserves and account-security features like two-factor authentication and withdrawal address allowlisting. None of that removes the regulatory gap, but it does mean the platform is not a small operation. As with any exchange, only keep trading funds on-platform, not long-term savings.

Who is Binance Australia for?

Binance suits two kinds of Australian users particularly well. First, active spot traders who value a low fee and deep liquidity above all else, and who are comfortable funding via card, P2P or a crypto transfer. Second, experienced users who already hold crypto and want the widest market to trade it in.

It is a worse fit for beginners who just want to buy Bitcoin with Australian dollars as simply as possible — for them, the busier interface and the card fee make a local option like Swyftx more practical. It's also not the choice for anyone who specifically wants locally licensed derivatives, which Binance simply doesn't offer in Australia. See which exchange fits your situation for the side-by-side.

Risk: reusing saved PayID or BSB details after Binance's AUD deposit status changed

The concrete mistake is paying Australian dollars to payment details you already have saved — inside your banking app, on an old transaction, or in a screenshot — instead of generating the instruction fresh in your own Binance account. Binance's direct AUD rails were frozen for roughly two years from mid-2023 when its local banking partner withdrew support, and the January 2026 restoration was rolled out in stages to verified customers. Deposit details and payee names can be different from what an older instruction shows, and money sent to details that are no longer live is not automatically returned to you.

This is unusually easy to get wrong here because Binance's own documentation has not been fully consistent: some help pages updated after the January 2026 announcement still mark BSB and PayID deposits as temporarily unavailable. When the platform's published status and your account's live status disagree, only one of them can actually move your money — and it is not the article, the forum post or the saved template.

To limit the risk, generate the deposit instruction inside your account every time, and check that the payee name shown on that screen matches the name you expect to be paying before you authorise anything. Send a small test amount first and confirm it lands and is credited before sending the balance. Never reuse details from an earlier transfer, and never act on payment details sent to you by someone who contacted you first.

If it has already gone wrong

Contact Binance support immediately with the transaction reference and a screenshot of the deposit screen you used, then call your Australian bank — a recall request is time-sensitive and works better the sooner it is raised. Do not send a second transfer to “correct” the first one.

Frequently asked questions

Is Binance legal in Australia?

Binance Australia is registered with AUSTRAC as a virtual asset service provider, so it is registered to provide digital currency exchange services. It does not currently hold an AFSL (an application is pending with ASIC), its former derivatives business was fined A$10 million in March 2026 over client misclassification, and direct PayID AUD deposits were reported as restored in January 2026, though some official support pages still show them as unavailable. Spot trading remains available; derivatives do not. For the full picture, read our guide on whether Binance is legal in Australia.

Can I deposit AUD into Binance Australia?

Binance announced the restoration of direct AUD deposits via PayID and bank transfer (BSB/account number) in January 2026, with zero-fee deposits published for these methods. However, some support pages still show BSB/PayID deposits as temporarily unavailable, so check which methods appear in your own account. Card purchases and P2P trading are also available, and you can transfer crypto in from another exchange or wallet.

What are Binance Australia's fees?

Standard spot trading is 0.10% maker/taker, dropping to 0.075% if you pay fees with BNB. Card purchases can include fees shown at checkout, while the effective P2P cost depends on the counterparty's quoted rate.

Does Binance have an AFSL in Australia?

Not currently. Binance's Australian operations are AUSTRAC-registered but do not currently hold an Australian Financial Services Licence, although Binance says an AFSL application is pending with ASIC. Its former derivatives business surrendered its licence in 2023, and derivatives are not available to Australian users.

Is Binance safe to use in Australia?

Binance is the world's largest exchange with substantial reserves and standard account-security features, but it operates in Australia without an AFSL and with limited direct AUD rails. Only keep trading funds on-platform and understand the regulatory position before you deposit.
