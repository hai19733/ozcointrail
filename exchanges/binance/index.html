Binance Australia Review 2026: Fees, AUD Deposits & Regulation

Last verified: 16 September 2026 · By OzCoinTrail Editorial Team

Binance has one of the lowest standard spot trading fees available to Australians, at 0.10% maker/taker with a 25% BNB fee discount reducing it to 0.075%, and the deepest order books of any exchange open to Australians. The trade-off is on the Australian dollar side: direct PayID and bank-transfer deposits are still temporarily unavailable, and Binance does not currently hold an AFSL. This review sets out exactly what that means for you, so you can decide whether Binance fits how you actually trade.

Verdict

Best for low-cost spot trading — with an AUD catch

Choose Binance if you want the lowest standard spot fee and the deepest liquidity in Australia, and you're comfortable funding your account via card, P2P or a crypto transfer instead of a direct PayID deposit. If free PayID AUD deposits or a locally licensed platform matter more to you, the trade-off may not be worth it — see how it compares in our full exchange comparison.

Fees explained

Binance's headline number is simple: a flat 0.10% maker and taker fee on standard spot pairs. Turn on “Use BNB to pay for fees” in your account settings and that drops 25% to 0.075%. At the volumes most retail traders actually hit, this is among the lowest standard spot fees offered by major exchanges commonly used in Australia — but note the fine print: the discount means holding BNB, and BNB's own price moves while you hold it.

Where Binance can get expensive is getting money in. Card purchases may include transaction or payment-provider charges shown at checkout, which can outweigh the 0.10% spot-fee advantage on smaller purchases. P2P pricing also depends on the merchant or counterparty quote, so compare the effective AUD-to-crypto rate rather than looking only at the headline trading fee.

AUD deposits & withdrawals: the PayID situation

This is the single most important thing to know about Binance in Australia right now. Binance's own help pages state that “AUD deposits via Bank Transfer (BSB / PayID) are temporarily unavailable”, and the exchange says it is actively working to restore full AUD services. Australian users currently fund accounts through the alternatives that remain available — such as card purchases or P2P trading, where PayID is used between buyers and sellers rather than as a direct deposit into Binance.

Don't confuse the two

Reports that Binance “restored PayID” in early 2026 are referring to P2P or older statements, not direct deposits. As at September 2026, direct AUD deposits via BSB or PayID are still marked temporarily unavailable on Binance's own support pages. If free PayID deposits matter to you, this is the deciding factor — platforms like CoinSpot and Swyftx have kept them running throughout.

Because direct AUD rails are limited, some Australians use a two-step path: buy on a local exchange with PayID, then transfer the crypto to Binance to trade. That adds a transfer fee and a little friction, but it lets you keep Binance's low spot fee while still funding with Australian dollars. Whether that's worth it depends on how much you move — for small amounts the extra hop usually isn't.

What you can trade

Binance offers spot trading, a P2P marketplace and Earn products to Australian users. The spot market is the core strength: deep liquidity across hundreds of coins means tighter spreads and less slippage than smaller local exchanges, especially outside the biggest pairs.

What you cannot do in Australia is trade derivatives. Binance's former derivatives business (operated through Oztures Trading Pty Ltd) surrendered its Australian financial services licence in 2023, and on 27 March 2026 the Federal Court ordered it to pay a A$10 million penalty after more than 85% of Australian clients were misclassified as wholesale over a nine-month period, exposing 524 retail clients to high-risk derivatives. Spot trading was not the subject of that action and remains available; derivatives are not.

Regulation & safety

Binance Australia is registered with AUSTRAC as a Virtual Asset Service Provider. It does not currently hold an AFSL (the ASIC licence that authorises specified financial services), although Binance says an AFSL application has been lodged with ASIC and remains pending determination. That distinction matters more in 2026 than it used to: Australia's new digital-asset framework received Royal Assent on 8 April 2026 and its main provisions commence on 8 April 2027, bringing digital asset platforms into a new licensing regime, and expanded Travel Rule obligations for covered providers started on 1 July 2026. Read our crypto regulation guide to understand where Binance sits under the new rules.

On operational security, Binance has the resources of the world's largest exchange behind it — including large asset reserves and account-security features like two-factor authentication and withdrawal address allowlisting. None of that removes the regulatory gap, but it does mean the platform is not a small operation. As with any exchange, only keep trading funds on-platform, not long-term savings.

Who is Binance Australia for?

Binance suits two kinds of Australian users particularly well. First, active spot traders who value a low fee and deep liquidity above all else, and who are comfortable funding via card, P2P or a crypto transfer. Second, experienced users who already hold crypto and want the widest market to trade it in.

It is a worse fit for beginners who just want to buy Bitcoin with Australian dollars as simply as possible — for them, the missing PayID deposit and the card fee make a local option more practical. It's also not the choice for anyone who specifically wants locally licensed derivatives, which Binance simply doesn't offer in Australia. See which exchange fits your situation for the side-by-side.

Frequently asked questions

Is Binance legal in Australia?

Binance Australia is registered with AUSTRAC as a virtual asset service provider, so it is registered to provide digital currency exchange services. It does not currently hold an AFSL (an application is pending with ASIC), its former derivatives business was fined A$10 million in March 2026 over client misclassification, and direct PayID AUD deposits are currently unavailable. Spot trading remains available; derivatives do not. For the full picture, read our guide on whether Binance is legal in Australia.

Can I deposit AUD into Binance Australia?

Direct AUD deposits via bank transfer (BSB / PayID) are temporarily unavailable, per Binance's own support pages. You can still fund an account through card purchases or P2P trading, or by transferring crypto in from another exchange or wallet.

What are Binance Australia's fees?

Standard spot trading is 0.10% maker/taker, dropping to 0.075% if you pay fees with BNB. Card purchases can include fees shown at checkout, while the effective P2P cost depends on the counterparty's quoted rate.

Does Binance have an AFSL in Australia?

Not currently. Binance's Australian operations are AUSTRAC-registered but do not currently hold an Australian Financial Services Licence, although Binance says an AFSL application is pending with ASIC. Its former derivatives business surrendered its licence in 2023, and derivatives are not available to Australian users.

Is Binance safe to use in Australia?

Binance is the world's largest exchange with substantial reserves and standard account-security features, but it operates in Australia without an AFSL and with limited direct AUD rails. Only keep trading funds on-platform and understand the regulatory position before you deposit.
