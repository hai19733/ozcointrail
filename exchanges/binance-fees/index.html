Binance Fees in Australia 2026: 0.10% Spot, Deposit & Withdrawal Costs

Last verified: 29 September 2026 · By James Walker · Editor, OzCoinTrail

If you're comparing Australian exchanges, Binance's 0.10% standard spot trading fee is among the lowest you'll find. But it comes with a catch for Australian users: direct AUD deposits via PayID or BSB bank transfer are still marked temporarily unavailable. The headline fee is cheap, but how you get Australian dollars into your account can add cost and friction. This guide breaks down Binance Australia's actual fees for spot trading, deposits, withdrawals and crypto transfers in 2026 — and how they compare with CoinSpot, OKX and Swyftx. Why 0.10% is among the lowest — but not unique

Binance's standard spot fee of 0.10% maker and 0.10% taker is one of the lowest standard rates in Australia. What matters for accuracy is the word among: CoinSpot's order-book Markets trading also charges 0.10%, so it would be wrong to say Binance is uniquely the cheapest. Where Binance genuinely stands out is liquidity — the depth of its order books tends to mean tighter spreads and better execution on popular pairs, which can matter as much as the headline fee.

The BNB discount: 25% off eligible spot fees

If you hold BNB and enable Use BNB to pay fees, Binance currently applies a 25% discount to eligible spot trading fees. For a regular user whose standard spot fee is 0.10%, that reduces the rate to 0.075% for both maker and taker orders.

Think of 0.075% as the discounted rate for a regular user, not a universal fee for every account. Higher-volume VIP users already have lower base fees, so the discount applies to their own lower rate; and Binance can also run pair-specific or promotional rates. Check the live fee shown in your account before a large trade. If you trade often, enabling the BNB discount is the simplest way to lower your effective cost without changing platforms.

AUD deposit fees — and the PayID gap

This is where Binance's fee picture gets more complicated for Australians. Direct AUD deposits via PayID or BSB bank transfer are currently marked temporarily unavailable on Binance's own support pages, as at September 2026. That means you generally cannot fund your account the same free, near-instant way you can on CoinSpot or Swyftx.

Some reports from early 2026 claimed Binance had “restored PayID”. Those reports describe P2P payments or older statements, not a return of direct deposits — so don't treat them as confirmation that free bank deposits are back. Binance itself says it is working to restore PayID services, but until the Deposit → AUD screen in your account offers a BSB/PayID option, direct deposits are not available to you. For a step-by-step on checking this in your account, see our guide on Binance Australia PayID deposits.

With direct deposits unavailable, Australians typically fund a Binance account through one of these routes:

  • Card — convenient, but a separate card fee applies and the exact amount varies, so check the figure shown before you confirm.
  • P2P — you buy from another user, often paying via PayID or bank transfer between users; Binance generally does not charge a P2P trading fee, but the counterparty's exchange rate sets your effective cost.
  • Crypto transfer — buy on a local exchange with free PayID, then send the crypto to Binance. This adds a network fee and a little friction, but keeps Binance's low spot fee while still funding with Australian dollars.

AUD withdrawal fees

AUD withdrawals need more nuance than simply saying they are free. Binance's own FAQ states that users who have never previously deposited AUD through the bank-transfer channel (BSB/PayID) may not be able to withdraw AUD the same way, because a prior deposit can be required to enable that route. A new withdrawal account can also require adding and verifying bank details, including a small test deposit. So if you are opening a fresh Binance Australia account in 2026, do not assume direct AUD bank withdrawal will appear automatically — check Withdraw → Fiat → AUD inside your account. Where bank withdrawal is unavailable, Binance points Australian users toward alternatives such as P2P.

Crypto deposit & withdrawal fees

Crypto deposits into Binance generally carry no network fee from Binance's side, although some assets or networks can have a minimum deposit amount below which the deposit may not be credited. Crypto withdrawals are different: there is no single fixed fee, because the amount varies by cryptocurrency, blockchain network and current network conditions. Binance shows the withdrawal fee before you confirm, so check the live preview rather than relying on a static table.

How to keep your Binance costs low

  1. Pay trading fees with BNB. If you hold BNB, enabling the BNB discount cuts your effective spot fee by 25%, from 0.10% to 0.075% for a regular user.
  2. Avoid card funding where you can. Card purchases carry a separate fee. Where practical, use P2P or a crypto transfer from a local exchange so more of your Australian dollars reach the trade itself.
  3. Check the card fee and exchange rate before confirming. The fee and the quoted rate are both shown before you confirm — compare the crypto you actually receive, not just the advertised percentage.
  4. Know your tier. Binance's VIP program can lower trading fees based on 30-day trading volume and BNB holdings, so frequent traders may already qualify for a lower rate than the standard 0.10%.

Frequently asked questions

What is Binance Australia's standard spot trading fee?

Direct AUD deposits via PayID or BSB bank transfer are currently marked temporarily unavailable. Australians generally fund their account via card, P2P or a crypto transfer, and card funding can carry a separate fee shown before you confirm.

No. Binance does not charge inactivity, account maintenance or custody fees.

There is no single fixed fee. It varies by cryptocurrency and blockchain network and is displayed before you confirm the withdrawal.
