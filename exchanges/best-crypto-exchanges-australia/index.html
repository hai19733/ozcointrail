Best Crypto Exchanges in Australia 2026: Binance, OKX, CoinSpot & Swyftx Compared

Last verified: 16 September 2026 · By OzCoinTrail Editorial Team

The best exchange for you in Australia comes down to how you'll actually use it. If you want the simplest way to buy and sell with Australian dollars and don't mind paying a little more for the convenience, CoinSpot or Swyftx are the natural starting points. If you want the lowest standard spot fee and deeper markets, Binance is the pick — while OKX is the one to look at for locally licensed derivatives, though its Australian fees are higher than its global rates. Here's how all four compare on fees, AUD deposits, regulation and support.

How to choose an exchange in Australia

Before you look at a single fee table, work out which of these matters most to you:

  • Fees — are you a buy-and-hold investor (instant-buy fees matter most) or an active trader (spot maker/taker fees matter most)?
  • AUD funding — do you need free PayID deposits, and how quickly does the money need to arrive?
  • Regulation — Australia's rules are changing in 2026, and an AUSTRAC registration is not the same thing as an ASIC licence.
  • Coin range — do you only want Bitcoin and a few majors, or a much wider list?
  • Local support — do you value Australian-based support during local business hours?

The four exchanges in detail

Binance Australia

Low-cost spot & depth

Binance runs the largest crypto market in the world, and that shows up as deep liquidity and a flat 0.10% spot fee (0.075% if you pay with BNB). The one to know about is AUD funding: direct PayID AUD deposits are currently unavailable. Australian users can still fund an account through supported alternatives such as card purchases or P2P trading — where PayID is used between buyers and sellers, not as a direct deposit into Binance.

The trade-off is regulatory. Binance operates in Australia through an AUSTRAC-registered local entity but does not hold an AFSL, and its former derivatives business was ordered by the Federal Court to pay a A$10 million penalty in March 2026 over failures that led to hundreds of retail clients being misclassified as wholesale. Spot trading remains available; derivatives do not. Read our full Binance Australia review for the details.

  • Best for: traders who want a low standard spot fee and deep liquidity.
  • Watch for: no AFSL, no direct PayID deposits, and a regulatory history worth understanding first.

OKX Australia

Licensed derivatives

OKX Australia runs a separate fee schedule from OKX Global. As at September 2026 the standard AU retail tier is 0.70% maker / taker — noticeably higher than the 0.08%/0.10% figures quoted on many sites for the global platform. Its real strength locally is regulation: the derivatives entity holds AFSL 379035, and a separate entity (AFSL 536984) covers the OKX Pay wallet and card.

Eligible retail clients can access crypto derivatives under the local licence, though leverage limits differ from OKX Global. Card purchases remain the expensive way to fund an account. See our OKX Australia review.

  • Best for: traders who want Australian-licensed derivatives and a broad product range.
  • Watch for: AU fees are higher than global rates, and features vary by verification level.

CoinSpot

Simplest & most local

CoinSpot is the most beginner-friendly local option and the one many Australians start with. It's AUSTRAC-registered, ISO 27001 certified, lists 510+ coins and offers 24/7 Australian-based support. PayID deposits are free, and both AUD and crypto withdrawals are free apart from network fees.

The catch is price. CoinSpot charges a 1% fee on Instant Buy, Sell and Swap, and the quoted execution price can differ from prices visible on order-book markets — so compare the final quote before confirming. Its “Markets” interface charges 0.1% on supported pairs but covers fewer coins with thinner liquidity. Read CoinSpot vs Binance to see the cost difference on a real trade.

  • Best for: beginners who want the simplest AUD experience and local support.
  • Watch for: the 1% instant-buy fee — the “cheap” 0.1% rate only applies on the Markets screen.

Swyftx

Local & lower cost

Swyftx sits between CoinSpot and the global exchanges: it's Brisbane-based and AUSTRAC-registered. Swyftx Pty Ltd also holds AFSL 568543, although its spot cryptocurrency exchange services are not provided under that AFSL. It uses a 1:1 asset-backing model and charges a 0.6% standard trading fee plus a separate spread. Free PayID, Osko and bank-transfer deposits are supported up to A$100,000 per day, and AUD withdrawals are free.

The 0.6% rate is the standard tier, and lower tiers down to 0.1% apply only at high 30-day volumes — the fee tier does not change the spread. For most retail users, Swyftx is a solid “local but cheaper” alternative to CoinSpot.

  • Best for: beginners to intermediate users who want local support at a lower cost than CoinSpot.
  • Watch for: the 0.6% fee is charged on top of the spread, not instead of it.

Regulation is changing in 2026 — read this before you deposit

An AUSTRAC registration and an ASIC licence are not the same thing. AUSTRAC covers anti-money-laundering obligations; an AFSL from ASIC authorises specified financial services. Australia's new digital-asset framework was passed on 1 April 2026 and commences on 9 April 2027, bringing digital asset platforms and tokenised custody platforms into a new financial-services licensing framework. From 1 July 2026, expanded Travel Rule obligations require covered virtual-asset service providers to collect and transmit prescribed originator and beneficiary information for relevant transfers.

Frequently asked questions

Which exchange has the lowest fees in Australia?

Among the four compared here, Binance's standard 0.10% spot fee and CoinSpot's 0.10% Markets fee are the lowest headline trading rates. They are not directly equivalent, however: CoinSpot's 0.10% rate applies only to supported Markets pairs, while its default Instant Buy, Sell and Swap service charges 1%.

Is Binance legal in Australia?

Binance operates through an AUSTRAC-registered local entity, so it is registered to provide digital currency exchange services. But it does not hold an AFSL, its former derivatives business was fined A$10 million in March 2026 over client misclassification, and direct PayID AUD deposits are currently unavailable. Spot trading remains available; derivatives do not.

Which exchange is best for beginners?

CoinSpot for the simplest AUD experience, or Swyftx if you want a lower-cost local option with the same convenience.

Can I deposit AUD with PayID?

CoinSpot, Swyftx and OKX support AUD deposits, and PayID is free on CoinSpot and Swyftx. Binance's direct PayID AUD deposits are currently unavailable — Australian users fund their account through card, P2P or crypto transfers instead. Card deposits are the expensive option everywhere.

Do I need to worry about the new AFSL rules?

The new regime takes effect 9 April 2027 with a transition period, so nothing changes overnight. For now, check a platform is AUSTRAC-registered and understand the difference between that and an AFSL before depositing.
