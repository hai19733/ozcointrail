OKX Australia Review 2026: Fees, PayID & Licensed Derivatives

Last verified: 16 September 2026 · By OzCoinTrail Editorial Team

OKX is often described as one of the cheaper global crypto exchanges, because its standard non-AUD spot fees start at 0.08% maker and 0.10% taker. For Australians, that headline number doesn't tell the whole story. If you're buying crypto directly with Australian dollars, OKX Australia currently charges regular retail users 0.70% maker and 0.70% taker on AUD-quoted spot pairs — considerably more than the crypto-to-crypto fee you'll see advertised. Where OKX stands out is elsewhere: working AUD deposits, a broad crypto and Web3 product set, and a derivatives business that operates through OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd under AFSL 379035. This review separates the AUD buying experience from OKX's non-AUD trading and locally licensed derivatives, so you can see where the platform is competitive — and where it isn't.

Choose OKX if you want to trade derivatives on a platform that holds an Australian Financial Services Licence, or if you already hold crypto and mostly trade non-AUD pairs where the 0.08% fee applies. If your plan is simply to buy Bitcoin with Australian dollars at the lowest cost, OKX's current 0.70% AUD-pair fee is materially higher than the 0.10% headline spot rates offered by some competing Australian platforms — see how the numbers line up in our full exchange comparison.

Fees explained: the number everyone gets wrong

OKX's fee structure in Australia depends on which pair you trade, and this is where most write-ups go astray. For regular retail accounts, OKX Australia charges 0.70% maker and 0.70% taker on AUD-quoted spot pairs — pairs like AUD/BTC or AUD/USDT, where one side of the trade is Australian dollars. On standard non-AUD pairs such as BTC/USDT, the fee is the 0.08% maker / 0.10% taker you see quoted everywhere.

Why the gap matters: most Australians' first trade is buying crypto with dollars, which happens on an AUD pair. That means the fee you actually pay for that transaction is 0.70% — not 0.08%. The low rate only kicks in once you're already holding crypto and trading one coin for another.

Why everyone quotes 0.08%

Many comparison sites take OKX's global fee schedule and present it as the Australian rate. OKX introduced a separate Australian fee framework in April 2026, and in August 2026 consolidated its ordinary token spot pairs into a standard group — but AUD fiat-quoted pairs were carved out and kept their own rates. The 0.08% figure is real, but only for non-AUD pairs. When a site calls OKX “the cheapest exchange in Australia” without that distinction, treat it with caution.

Put against the local alternatives, the picture flips depending on the pair. On AUD pairs, OKX's 0.70% is materially higher than Binance's 0.10% (or 0.075% with BNB) and CoinSpot Markets' 0.10%. On non-AUD major pairs, OKX's 0.08% is marginally cheaper than Binance's 0.10% — but that only helps you if you're already moving between coins. Note that OKX's quick buy / convert option is separate again: like most instant-buy features, it may include a spread on top of the quoted rate, so compare the final AUD amount before you confirm.

AUD deposits & withdrawals

On the dollar side, OKX is in a better position than Binance right now. OKX Australia supports AUD deposits via bank transfer and PayID, and OKX currently states that no fees are being charged on AUD withdrawals to an Australian bank account. Deposit fees vary by method — OKX shows the applicable fee on the deposit preview in your account, so check there before you confirm. That's still a meaningful difference from Binance, whose direct PayID deposits are marked temporarily unavailable, so if you want to move Australian dollars in and out without an extra hop, OKX clears that bar.

As with any exchange, the practical cost of moving AUD is not always the headline fee. Quick-buy and conversion features can embed a spread, and the exchange rate you're quoted on an AUD pair will move with the market. For larger dollar amounts, comparing the effective AUD-per-coin rate you're offered against a couple of alternatives is worth the two minutes it takes.

What you can trade

OKX offers spot trading, derivatives, a Web3 wallet, trading bots and a demo-trading environment to Australian users, with 370+ supported tokens as of July 2026. The broad coin range and built-in Web3 tooling are part of what separates OKX from simpler local platforms.

The derivatives offering is the real headline for Australian traders, because it's the area where OKX holds an Australian licence. Retail clients can access derivatives on eligible products up to 2x leverage, subject to suitability and knowledge assessments, while wholesale or professional clients can go considerably higher — up to 50x on some products. If you've previously traded derivatives offshore, the leverage limits available to Australian retail clients here are more conservative than the global platform's.

Regulation & safety

OKX operates in Australia through three entities, and it's worth knowing which one you're dealing with:

  • OKX Australia Pty Ltd provides spot digital currency exchange services and is registered with AUSTRAC. Spot trading is not covered by an AFSL.
  • OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd holds AFSL 379035 and issues the derivatives and margin products.
  • Bano Pty Ltd holds AFSL 536984 and provides the OKX Pay wallet and card.

That AFSL 379035 is the distinction to focus on: it's the reason OKX is one of the few global exchanges that can offer derivatives to Australians under an Australian licence, where Binance's former derivatives arm exited the market after surrendering its licence in 2023. It does not mean spot trading is AFSL-regulated — that remains an AUSTRAC registration — but the derivatives side is genuinely locally licensed.

The licensing backdrop is changing too. Australia's Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 8 April 2026, with the main digital-asset platform licensing provisions scheduled to commence on 8 April 2027. The framework will bring qualifying digital asset platforms into Australia's financial-services licensing regime. Australia's expanded AML/CTF rules for virtual asset services are also being phased in, with certain new virtual asset services becoming subject to obligations including Travel Rule requirements from 1 July 2026. On operational security, OKX publishes regular proof-of-reserves and offers the standard account protections such as two-factor authentication — but as with any exchange, keep only trading funds on-platform rather than long-term savings.

Who is OKX Australia for?

OKX fits two groups particularly well. First, traders who want derivatives on a locally licensed platform — that's the genuine differentiator, and it's the reason to choose OKX over Binance: Binance Australia does not currently offer the locally licensed retail crypto derivatives proposition that OKX Australia provides. Second, experienced users who already hold crypto and spend most of their time trading non-AUD pairs, where the 0.08% fee and the wide coin range earn their keep.

It's a poorer fit for beginners who simply want to buy Bitcoin with Australian dollars at the lowest cost. For that one trade, OKX's 0.70% AUD-pair fee is materially higher than what Binance or CoinSpot Markets charge, and the platform's depth of features is more than most first-time buyers need. If you're comparing your options, start with which exchange fits your situation before committing.

Frequently asked questions

Is OKX legal in Australia?

Yes. OKX operates through three Australian entities: OKX Australia Pty Ltd (AUSTRAC-registered spot exchange), OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd (AFSL 379035 for derivatives and margin products) and Bano Pty Ltd (AFSL 536984 for OKX Pay). Spot trading is AUSTRAC-registered rather than AFSL-licensed, while the derivatives business holds an Australian Financial Services Licence.

What are OKX Australia's fees?

For regular retail accounts, OKX charges 0.70% maker and taker on AUD-quoted spot pairs, and 0.08% maker / 0.10% taker on standard non-AUD pairs such as BTC/USDT. AUD deposit fees vary by method — check the fee on the deposit preview — while OKX currently charges no fee on AUD withdrawals. Quick-buy and conversion features may include a spread on top of the quoted rate.

Is OKX cheaper than Binance in Australia?

It depends on the pair. On standard non-AUD pairs, OKX's 0.08% is marginally cheaper than Binance's 0.10%. On AUD pairs — where Australians actually buy crypto with dollars — OKX's 0.70% is materially higher than Binance's 0.10%, so Binance is the cheaper option for that transaction.

Can I deposit AUD into OKX Australia?

Yes. OKX Australia supports AUD deposits via bank transfer and PayID — deposit fees vary by method and are shown on the deposit preview — and OKX currently charges no fee on AUD withdrawals to an Australian bank account. This is a difference from Binance, whose direct PayID deposits are currently marked temporarily unavailable.

Does OKX have an AFSL in Australia?

Its derivatives and margin business does — OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd holds AFSL 379035 — and OKX Pay sits under Bano Pty Ltd's AFSL 536984. Spot trading itself is AUSTRAC-registered but not covered by an AFSL.

Can I trade derivatives on OKX in Australia?

Yes, with limits. Retail clients can access derivatives on eligible products up to 2x leverage, while wholesale or professional clients can go up to 50x on some products. These Australian retail limits are more conservative than OKX's global platform.
