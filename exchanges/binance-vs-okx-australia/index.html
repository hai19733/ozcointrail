Binance vs OKX Australia 2026: Fees, Safety & Which Is Better

Last verified: 27 September 2026 · By James Walker · Editor, OzCoinTrail

If you're comparing Binance and OKX in Australia, there is one difference you should know before anything else: Binance currently charges a standard 0.10% spot trading fee, while a regular OKX Australia retail account pays 0.70% maker and 0.70% taker on standard spot pairs.

That matters because many Binance vs OKX comparisons quote OKX's better-known global fee schedule and assume Australians get the same rates. They don't.

But fees are only part of the comparison. OKX currently has the clearer local AUD bank-transfer route and offers regulated crypto perpetual derivatives to eligible Australian retail clients with leverage capped at 2x. Binance Australia, meanwhile, does not currently offer derivatives, and its official support page still says AUD deposits by BSB/PayID are temporarily unavailable.

So there isn't one answer for every Australian user.

The fee difference most comparison guides miss

This is the part worth checking carefully.

As of 27 September 2026, Binance's Regular User spot fee schedule starts at:

  • 0.10% maker
  • 0.10% taker
  • around 0.075% when the eligible 25% BNB fee discount is applied

OKX Australia uses a separate local retail fee structure. For a Regular retail user, its current standard spot schedule is 0.70% maker and 0.70% taker. That Australian framework took effect on 22 April 2026. We flag the same mismatch in our OKX Australia review.

What does that mean in dollars?

On an A$1,000 spot trade, ignoring spread and any promotional fee exemptions:

  • Binance at 0.10% ≈ A$1
  • OKX Australia at 0.70% ≈ A$7

So at the Regular retail tier, OKX's headline trading fee is about seven times Binance's standard spot fee.

That does not mean every trade will cost exactly those amounts. Your final cost can also depend on the pair you trade, whether the pair has a temporary zero-fee promotion, the bid/ask spread, your VIP level, and the execution price of your order. Still, the base fee difference is large enough that Australian users should not ignore it.

Why you keep seeing OKX quoted as 0.08% / 0.10%

Search for "OKX fees" and you'll often see figures around 0.08% maker / 0.10% taker. Those figures should not automatically be applied to an Australian Regular retail account.

OKX Australia's current local fee page lists 0.70% maker / 0.70% taker for Regular users on standard spot pairs. Higher-volume traders can move down through the VIP tiers:

Derivatives: this is where OKX is very different

If you only buy Bitcoin or altcoins on the spot market, derivatives may not matter. If you want leveraged trading, however, Binance and OKX Australia are currently very different products.

OKX Australia

OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd holds AFSL 379035 for its derivatives business. Eligible retail clients can access USDT-margined perpetual contracts with leverage of up to 2:1. Before retail derivatives are enabled, OKX requires users to complete both a suitability assessment and a knowledge assessment. Its current knowledge test contains 12 questions, with at least 10 correct answers required to proceed.

OKX also states that retail clients receive protections including negative-balance protection. That does not make leveraged trading low risk. A position can still be fully liquidated, and OKX explicitly warns that a user may lose all USDT placed in the derivatives trading account. For the full licensing picture, see Is OKX legal in Australia.

Binance Australia

Binance no longer operates its previous Australian derivatives service. Its former Australian derivatives entity, Oztures Trading Pty Ltd, had its AFS licence cancelled in 2023 after requesting cancellation. In March 2026, the Federal Court ordered Oztures Trading — which traded as Binance Australia Derivatives — to pay a A$10 million penalty over failures involving the classification and onboarding of retail clients between 2022 and 2023. ASIC said 524 retail investors had been exposed to high-risk derivatives without required retail protections.

This enforcement case relates to the former derivatives operation, not Binance Australia's current spot exchange service. See Is Binance legal in Australia for the distinction.

Derivatives verdict

For eligible Australian retail clients who specifically want crypto perpetual derivatives, OKX currently offers a regulated Australian product; Binance Australia does not offer an equivalent retail derivatives service.

Is Binance regulated in Australia?

Yes, but you need to understand what type of registration it has. Binance Australia states that it is registered with AUSTRAC as a Virtual Asset Service Provider under registration number 100576141-001. It also says it has lodged an application with ASIC for an Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL) and that the application remains pending.

That distinction matters: AUSTRAC registration is not the same thing as holding an AFSL. AUSTRAC focuses primarily on Australia's anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorism-financing framework, while an AFSL applies to regulated financial services and financial products where Australian financial-services law requires one. So avoid treating "Binance is AUSTRAC registered" as equivalent to "Binance holds an AFSL covering its Australian spot exchange" — they are not the same statement.

Is OKX regulated in Australia?

OKX's Australian structure also needs to be separated by product. Its derivatives are issued by OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd, ABN 14 145 724 509, AFSL 379035. That AFSL relates to the derivatives products offered through that entity.

It does not mean every crypto service on OKX is covered by the AFSL in exactly the same way. This is an important distinction because "regulated exchange" is often used too broadly in crypto marketing. When comparing platforms, check which legal entity provides the product, what licence or registration that entity holds, and whether that licence actually covers the product you intend to use.

Safety: Binance vs OKX

Neither exchange should be treated like an Australian bank account. Crypto held on a centralised exchange remains exposed to counterparty risk: you rely on the exchange to safeguard assets, process withdrawals and continue operating normally.

Both Binance and OKX provide common account-security features such as two-factor authentication, withdrawal controls, device management, anti-phishing protections, and proof-of-reserves disclosures. But proof of reserves is not the same thing as government deposit insurance, and it does not remove every form of exchange risk. For long-term holdings, users who understand private-key security may also consider self-custody rather than leaving their entire portfolio permanently on any exchange.

Binance vs OKX for beginners

For a beginner, don't compare platforms only by the number of features they advertise. Start with the path you will actually use.

If you mainly want to buy and sell crypto

Binance's 0.10% standard spot fee is substantially below OKX Australia's 0.70% Regular retail rate. For repeated spot trading, that difference can add up quickly.

If you want to deposit AUD directly from a bank

OKX currently has the cleaner proposition. Binance's own Australian support documentation continues to list BSB/PayID deposits as temporarily unavailable.

If you want leverage

OKX is currently the relevant comparison. Eligible Australian retail users can access perpetual derivatives with leverage capped at 2x after passing the required assessments.

If you're simply buying Bitcoin and holding it

The most important factors may instead be total purchase cost, spread, AUD funding convenience, withdrawal fees, account security, and whether you intend to withdraw your Bitcoin to your own wallet.

Binance vs OKX Australia: bottom line

The Australian versions of Binance and OKX are more different than their global fee pages suggest.

Binance: 0.10% standard spot maker/taker fee, eligible BNB fee discount, AUSTRAC registered, AFSL application currently pending, no current Australian retail derivatives offering, and BSB/PayID AUD deposits currently listed as temporarily unavailable.

OKX Australia: 0.70% maker/taker for Regular retail spot trading, lower fees available through VIP tiers, local AUD funding options, regulated perpetual derivatives through OKX Australia Financial, up to 2x leverage for eligible retail clients, and integrated Web3 tools.

So the practical question isn't simply "Is Binance or OKX better?" — it's "What am I actually using the exchange for?" A user focused on low-cost spot trading faces a very different trade-off from someone who needs a straightforward AUD bank-transfer route or regulated derivatives. Check the fee and deposit screens in your own account before moving money, because exchange fees, promotions and payment methods can change.

Frequently asked questions

Is Binance cheaper than OKX in Australia?

For a Regular retail user's standard spot trades, yes on the current published fee schedules. Binance's standard spot fee is 0.10% maker / 0.10% taker, while OKX Australia currently lists 0.70% maker / 0.70% taker for its Regular retail tier.

Is OKX really 0.70% in Australia?

For a Regular OKX Australia retail account on standard spot pairs, the current published fee schedule is 0.70% maker and 0.70% taker. Higher VIP tiers receive lower fees, and designated zero-fee pairs or promotions may be treated differently.

Why do other websites say OKX charges 0.08% maker and 0.10% taker?

Those figures are commonly associated with OKX's broader/global fee structure and should not automatically be applied to an Australian Regular retail account. OKX Australia has its own fee framework.

Does Binance have an AFSL in Australia?

Binance Australia currently states that its AFSL application is pending determination. It is separately registered with AUSTRAC as a Virtual Asset Service Provider under registration number 100576141-001.

Does OKX have an AFSL?

OKX Australia Financial Pty Ltd holds AFSL 379035 and issues OKX's Australian crypto perpetual derivatives. That licence should not be interpreted as meaning every OKX spot-crypto service is covered by an AFSL in the same way.

Can Australians trade derivatives on Binance?

Binance Australia does not currently offer its former Australian retail derivatives service. Its previous derivatives operation was closed, and ASIC's 2026 enforcement action concerned failures in that former business.

Can Australians trade derivatives on OKX?

Eligible Australian retail users can access OKX USDT-margined perpetual contracts with leverage of up to 2x, after completing suitability and knowledge assessments.

Can I deposit AUD using PayID on Binance?

Binance's current Australian support page says AUD deposits through Bank Transfer (BSB / PayID) are temporarily unavailable. Because payment channels can change, check the deposit page inside your Binance account before relying on an older guide.

Which is better for an Australian beginner?

It depends on the task. Binance currently has the lower standard spot trading fee, while OKX currently provides the clearer local AUD bank-transfer route. Someone interested in regulated leveraged trading also has a materially different choice, because OKX offers eligible retail clients up to 2x perpetual derivatives under its Australian derivatives structure. For a first purchase, compare the total cost of funding + spread + trading fee + withdrawal fee, rather than choosing an exchange from the headline fee alone.
