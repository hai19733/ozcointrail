CoinSpot Review 2026: Fees, Safety & What Its AFSL Actually Covers

Last verified: 19 September 2026 · By James Walker · Editor, OzCoinTrail

CoinSpot is one of Australia's longest-running crypto exchanges and remains one of the easiest ways for Australians to move from AUD into crypto. PayID and direct bank deposits are free, support is local, and the platform lists hundreds of cryptocurrencies.

The main catch is cost.

CoinSpot's standard Instant Buy, Sell and Swap fee is 1%, while trades placed through its Markets order book cost 0.10%. For someone buying A$1,000 of Bitcoin, that difference alone can turn a trading fee of A$10 into about A$1 before considering the actual execution price.

There's also an important regulatory detail that is easy to misunderstand. CoinSpot's operator, Casey Block Services Pty Ltd, holds AFSL 562554, but that licence relates to its role in the CoinSpot Mastercard — it does not mean ordinary spot-crypto trades on CoinSpot are covered by the AFSL.

Verdict

Best for beginners who want a simple AUD on-ramp — but learn where the 0.10% Markets tab is

CoinSpot makes the most sense for Australians who value straightforward AUD deposits, a simple interface and local support. The important trick is to avoid using Instant Buy automatically: for supported major cryptocurrencies, check the Markets section first, where the headline trading fee is 0.10% instead of 1%.

If you're an active trader, need deeper order books or want derivatives, the trade-offs are different — our Australian crypto exchange comparison covers those options separately.

Fees explained: the 1% default you can avoid

CoinSpot has two very different fee tiers depending on which part of the app you use, and the cheaper one sits one screen deeper.

  • Instant Buy / Sell / Swap — 1%. This is the default option most people hit first, and the fee applies to every purchase made through it.
  • Markets trading fee — 0.10%. This is the order-book option, and it only applies to cryptocurrencies that have a dedicated AUD market on CoinSpot.
  • OTC — 0.10% for larger trades, and Take Profit / Stop / Limit orders — 1%.

The 1% trap, explained

Most beginners tap Instant Buy because it's the first button they see, and pay 1% on every purchase as a result. The 0.10% Markets fee only applies to cryptocurrencies that have a dedicated AUD market on CoinSpot, and CoinSpot's order book has lower liquidity than a global exchange like Binance — so on coins without a dedicated market you may only have the Instant Buy option anyway. Before you confirm any trade, compare the final AUD amount against the order-book price: the rate quoted on Instant Buy is not the same as the Markets price.

Put against the alternatives, the picture depends on which entry you use. CoinSpot's 0.10% Markets rate matches Binance's standard spot fee on supported markets, although the platforms differ substantially in liquidity, available pairs and AUD funding options. AUD deposits & withdrawals

CoinSpot's dollar rails are a genuine strength. Deposits via PayID and direct bank deposit are free, AUD withdrawals to an Australian bank account are free, and crypto withdrawals are charged only the network (blockchain) fee. That's the opposite of Binance's current position, where direct PayID deposits remain temporarily unavailable — if moving Australian dollars in and out without friction matters to you, CoinSpot clears that bar comfortably.

Other funding methods carry fees. CoinSpot's main fee schedule currently lists PayPal deposits at 0.5%, cash deposits at 2.5%, and a separate fee for card funding. Check the confirmation screen before paying, as funding fees can change. For the cheapest route, use a free PayID or direct deposit and then place a Markets order rather than an Instant Buy. As always, the effective AUD-per-coin rate you're offered is what matters, not the headline fee on its own.

What you can trade

CoinSpot is a spot-only exchange: no margin, no leverage, no futures, no derivatives and no crypto lending. For some people that's a feature — fewer ways to get into trouble — and for others it's a dealbreaker, because there's simply no way to trade with leverage here.

What CoinSpot does offer is breadth. It supports several hundred coins and describes itself as offering Australia's largest variety, though the exact count differs across its own pages (you'll see figures from 400+ to 500+ depending on where you look). On top of spot trading there's staking, “Bundles” (basket purchases), an NFT marketplace and the CoinSpot Mastercard for spending crypto.

Regulation & safety

CoinSpot is registered with AUSTRAC as a digital currency exchange, a registration it has held since May 2018. It also holds AFSL 562554 through Casey Block Services Pty Ltd — but read the scope carefully, because this is where most write-ups go wrong.

The AFSL fine print

CoinSpot's AFSL does not mean its crypto exchange is ASIC-licensed in the same way as a conventional financial product platform. Casey Block Services Pty Ltd holds AFSL 562554, but CoinSpot's own Financial Services Guide states that the licensed services relate to promoting and arranging the CoinSpot Mastercard and providing general financial product advice about the card. The Mastercard itself is issued by EML Payment Solutions Limited.

CoinSpot's crypto exchange activities are separately subject to Australia's AUSTRAC registration and AML/CTF framework. AUSTRAC registration and an AFSL serve different regulatory purposes, so you should not treat the Mastercard-related AFSL as regulatory coverage for ordinary spot-crypto trading.

On security, CoinSpot states it holds the majority of client assets in cold storage and cites third-party audits of one-to-one asset backing, and it says it was the first Australian exchange to achieve ISO 27001 certification. In November 2023, blockchain researchers reported that roughly US$2.4 million was drained from a known CoinSpot hot wallet. Security firm CertiK assessed the incident as a probable private-key compromise. Public reporting did not establish that individual customer balances were affected. None of that removes the usual exchange risk, so keep only what you're actively trading on-platform rather than long-term savings. Who is CoinSpot for?

CoinSpot is the right call for beginners who want to buy crypto with Australian dollars as simply as possible and value local support over fees. It's also a reasonable home for buy-and-hold investors who buy coins that have a dedicated AUD market and use the Markets order book rather than the Instant Buy button.

It's a worse fit for active traders chasing the lowest fees and deepest liquidity — for them, Binance's 0.10% spot fee with deeper order books is the closer comparison, and our Binance vs CoinSpot breakdown sets out the trade-off. Frequently asked questions

Is CoinSpot safe and legal in Australia?

CoinSpot is an AUSTRAC-registered digital currency exchange, with that registration dating back to May 2018, and it has operated since 2013. It also states it was the first Australian exchange to achieve ISO 27001 certification and holds the majority of client assets in cold storage. Like any exchange, it's not a bank: there's no government compensation scheme, so only keep trading funds on-platform.

Does CoinSpot have an AFSL?

It holds AFSL 562554 through Casey Block Services Pty Ltd, but CoinSpot's own Financial Services Guide states that the licensed services relate to promoting and arranging the CoinSpot Mastercard and providing general financial product advice about the card — not spot crypto trading. The Mastercard itself is issued by EML Payment Solutions Limited, and CoinSpot's exchange activities sit under its AUSTRAC registration instead.

What are CoinSpot's fees?

Instant Buy / Sell / Swap charges a flat 1%, while trading through CoinSpot Markets costs 0.10% on cryptocurrencies that have a dedicated AUD market, and OTC trades are 0.10%. PayID and direct bank deposits are free, AUD withdrawals are free, and crypto withdrawals cost only the network fee.

How do I avoid CoinSpot's 1% fee?

Use CoinSpot Markets where the cryptocurrency you want to trade has an AUD market available, and the trading fee is 0.10% instead of 1%. Coins without a dedicated AUD market may only be available through Instant Buy.

Is CoinSpot better than Binance?

It depends on what you value. CoinSpot is simpler, with free PayID and direct bank deposits and local support, while Binance offers the same 0.10% spot fee with deeper liquidity and more pairs — but its direct PayID deposits are currently unavailable. Beginners who want simplicity tend to prefer CoinSpot; active traders who want depth tend to prefer Binance. Our Binance vs CoinSpot guide goes into the detail.

Can I deposit AUD into CoinSpot for free?

Yes — CoinSpot offers free AUD deposits via PayID and direct bank deposit, and free AUD withdrawals to an Australian bank account. Card purchases, PayPal and cash deposits carry fees listed on CoinSpot's fee page.
