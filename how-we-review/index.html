How We Review

This page explains how OzCoinTrail researches, checks and assesses the cryptocurrency exchanges, transfer methods and services we write about.

Our aim is to give Australian readers enough evidence and context to make their own judgement — rather than reducing every platform to a single score.

Our approach

OzCoinTrail reviews are qualitative rather than based on a single numerical rating.

We do not give exchanges a score out of ten because the right platform can depend on how someone intends to use it. An exchange that works well for occasional AUD purchases may not be the best fit for an active trader, while a platform with advanced trading tools may be unnecessarily complex for a beginner.

Instead, we aim to:

  • explain clearly what a platform does well and where its limitations are;
  • focus on factors that matter specifically to Australian users;
  • separate verified facts from our editorial observations; and
  • explain which types of users a platform may suit, rather than declaring one exchange the “best” for everyone.

What we evaluate

When we review an exchange or compare platforms, we generally assess the following areas.

Fees and real cost

We look beyond the headline trading fee.

Depending on the platform, we may examine:

  • spot trading fees;
  • AUD and non-AUD trading pairs;
  • deposit and withdrawal fees;
  • crypto withdrawal fees;
  • spreads or quoted prices where relevant; and
  • other costs that may affect the amount a user actually pays.

Where possible, we distinguish published fees from costs that can vary with market conditions.

AUD deposits and withdrawals

For Australian users, moving Australian dollars on and off a platform is an important part of the experience.

We check factors such as:

  • PayID and bank-transfer support;
  • available AUD deposit methods;
  • processing times stated by the provider;
  • minimum or maximum amounts where relevant; and
  • whether the platform charges for AUD deposits or withdrawals.

Availability can depend on the user, bank, payment provider and account status, so we do not assume every funding method is available to everyone.

Australian regulatory status

We check relevant Australian regulatory records and explain what those records do — and do not — mean.

This may include whether a provider appears on AUSTRAC’s Virtual Asset Service Provider register and, where relevant to the services being offered, whether an entity holds or operates under an Australian Financial Services licence.

We treat these as different regulatory arrangements.

AUSTRAC registration relates primarily to obligations under Australia’s anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing framework. It is not the same as holding an Australian Financial Services licence and should not be treated as a government endorsement of an exchange.

Where licensing requirements depend on the particular product or service being offered, we say so rather than describing the entire platform simply as “licensed” or “unlicensed”.

Security and custody

We look at information the platform publishes about matters such as:

  • account-security features;
  • withdrawal protections;
  • custody arrangements;
  • storage practices;
  • proof-of-reserves or similar disclosures, where offered; and
  • significant publicly documented security incidents.

There are limits to what an external publisher can verify. We do not audit exchange code, wallets, reserves, internal controls or financial statements ourselves.

Where a security claim comes from the platform rather than an independent source, we identify it accordingly.

Products and features

Depending on the exchange, we may examine:

  • spot trading;
  • available crypto assets;
  • recurring purchases;
  • staking or earn products;
  • margin or derivatives products;
  • wallets;
  • cards; and
  • other major platform features.

We also consider whether particular products are actually available to Australian users rather than assuming that everything shown on a platform’s global website is available in Australia.

Ease of use and support

We consider practical parts of using a platform, including:

  • account creation;
  • identity verification;
  • AUD funding;
  • the app and website experience;
  • navigation;
  • access to help documentation; and
  • available customer-support channels.

Where we have directly tested a process or interface, we may say so in the relevant article. Where we have not personally tested something, we rely on documented sources rather than presenting it as first-hand experience.

Where we get our information

We give preference to primary sources.

These may include:

  • official exchange websites and apps;
  • fee schedules;
  • help-centre articles;
  • terms and conditions;
  • product disclosures;
  • AUSTRAC records and guidance;
  • ASIC registers and guidance;
  • Australian Taxation Office guidance; and
  • other Australian government or regulatory sources where relevant.

We may use reputable secondary sources for additional context, but we do not rely on a single news article, comparison website or affiliate page as the basis for an important factual claim.

When sources conflict, we generally give greater weight to the relevant regulator, legislation, formal terms or the provider’s current official documentation.

How we verify facts

Cryptocurrency services change frequently.

Fees, deposit methods, supported assets, withdrawal networks, eligibility requirements and regulatory arrangements can all change after an article is published.

For information that may materially affect a reader’s decision, we aim to check the current position against an appropriate primary source before publication or a substantive update.

Depending on the article, this can include checking:

  1. the platform’s current Australian website or app;
  2. its current fee or support documentation;
  3. relevant Australian regulatory records; and
  4. the actual transaction or account flow where practical.

Where we have performed a first-hand test, we explain what was tested and when rather than implying that every feature on the platform was independently tested.

Dates and updates

Time-sensitive articles display a Last reviewed, Last updated or Last verified date.

We update that date when we have materially reviewed the article or rechecked important information. We do not change the date simply to make an old article appear fresh.

A newer review date does not guarantee that every fee, limit or feature will remain unchanged after publication.

If a decision depends on a specific fee, eligibility requirement, transaction limit or regulatory status, check the platform or relevant regulator before acting.

Editorial independence

OzCoinTrail earns revenue through affiliate partnerships with some of the platforms mentioned on this website.

Those commercial relationships do not determine our conclusions.

We do not:

  • rank a platform more favourably because it pays a higher commission;
  • remove or deliberately soften a material negative finding because a platform is an affiliate partner;
  • accept payment in exchange for a positive review; or
  • guarantee that an affiliate platform is suitable for every Australian user.

Affiliate relationships are disclosed separately from our editorial assessment.

For more information, read our Affiliate Disclosure.

Corrections

Despite our checks, mistakes can happen and information can change.

If we find a material factual error, we correct it. If you believe something on OzCoinTrail is inaccurate or outdated, please tell us which page and which statement you are referring to so we can review it against the relevant sources.

See our Contact page to report a correction.

Our limitations

OzCoinTrail is an independent information website. We are not a cryptocurrency exchange, regulator, auditor or licensed financial adviser.

In particular:

  • we do not audit an exchange’s source code, reserves, wallets or internal systems;
  • we cannot independently verify every statement made by a platform;
  • we cannot guarantee that third-party services or published information will remain unchanged after our review;
  • access to products and payment methods may differ between users; and
  • our content is general information and does not take into account your personal objectives, financial situation or needs.

Nothing in our reviews should be treated as a guarantee that an exchange, asset or service is safe or suitable for you.

For more information about the scope of our content, read our Disclaimer.
