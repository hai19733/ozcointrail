How to Transfer Crypto from CoinSpot to Binance Australia

Last verified: 17 September 2026 · By OzCoinTrail Editorial Team

Sending crypto from CoinSpot to Binance Australia is a standard withdrawal on one side and a standard deposit on the other. CoinSpot charges a transaction (mining) fee when you send crypto to an external wallet — the amount depends on the coin and network and is shown before you confirm — while Binance does not charge to receive crypto deposits. The network has to match on both sides, and since 1 July 2026 Binance may ask for sender details before a deposit is credited.

The network is the whole game

A CoinSpot-to-Binance transfer isn't really one transfer — it's a coin and network combination. When you generate a deposit address on Binance you choose both a coin and a network. A USDT deposit on Ethereum (ERC20), Tron (TRC20) and BNB Smart Chain (BEP20) uses different blockchain networks. Even if two networks happen to display the same-looking address, you still need to select the exact same network on both platforms. Pick a network on Binance, then tell CoinSpot to send on a different one, and the coins either don't arrive or land on the wrong network — which can be slow or impossible to undo.

Binance also warns users to make sure the deposit network matches the network used by the sending platform. In practice, this is the most important check in the entire transfer. Everything after it is straightforward.

Two checks before you send anything

1. The network must match on both sides. The network you selected on Binance (when you copied the address) and the network you select on CoinSpot (when you send) have to be the same one.

2. Watch for a destination tag, memo or payment ID. Some assets require one in addition to the address. If Binance displays one on the deposit page, copy it exactly and enter it in the corresponding field on CoinSpot.

Step by step

Do the Binance side first, because the address and network you copy there determine everything CoinSpot asks for next.

On Binance: get the deposit address

  1. Log in and open Wallet, then Fiat and Spot (or hit Deposit).
  2. Choose the coin you're moving — for example USDT.
  3. Choose the network. Note which one you picked.
  4. Binance shows a deposit address. Copy it. If the coin uses a tag or memo, copy that as well.

On CoinSpot: send the coins

  1. Open Wallets, find the coin, and choose Send.
  2. Paste the Binance address into the recipient / payment address field.
  3. Select the network. CoinSpot shows a network option when you send — select the same network you chose on Binance rather than relying on the default network shown by CoinSpot.
  4. If the coin needs a tag or memo, enter it.
  5. Enter the amount, review the total (including the network fee CoinSpot displays), and confirm. CoinSpot will ask for two-factor authentication and send a confirmation email to complete the send.

Send a small amount first

If it's your first transfer on a given coin or network, send a small test amount and confirm it arrives in Binance before moving the rest. A test transaction costs another CoinSpot transaction/mining fee, but it reduces the risk of sending the full amount using the wrong network or tag.

After sending: check Binance for the sender-information prompt

Since 1 July 2026, Binance Australia may ask for additional sender information when crypto is deposited into your account. After CoinSpot broadcasts the transaction, open your Binance Deposit History. If the deposit shows as pending, open it and complete any sender/originator information Binance requests.

Binance says Australian users may be asked for details including the sender's full name, country of residence, unique identifier and city or locality. If the requested information isn't provided, the deposit can be delayed or, in some cases, returned to the originating exchange.

What it costs

CoinSpot charges a transaction (mining) fee when you send crypto to an external wallet. The amount depends on the coin and network, and CoinSpot shows it before you confirm — so check the final amount. Binance does not charge a fee to receive a crypto deposit.

If you bought the asset specifically to make this transfer, your overall cost may include the CoinSpot trading fee (1% on an Instant Buy, 0.1% on Markets) plus CoinSpot's transaction/mining fee for sending the asset. There's no separate Binance deposit fee on top.

How long it takes

That depends on the network and on how many confirmations Binance requires before it credits your account. On faster networks it's often a matter of minutes; on a busy chain it can take noticeably longer. Watch Binance's deposit / transaction history rather than refreshing CoinSpot — that's where the incoming deposit and its progress appear.

Why people make this move — and when it's worth it

The pattern that makes CoinSpot-to-Binance worthwhile is using CoinSpot as the AUD on-ramp and Binance as the trading venue. CoinSpot is where free PayID deposits and free AUD withdrawals live, and it's beginner-friendly. Binance is where the deeper order books and a much larger coin list live. Its standard spot fee for a regular user is 0.10%, before any applicable BNB discount or VIP rate.

This isn't about dodging CoinSpot's trading fee — CoinSpot's Markets already runs at 0.1%. It's about depth and selection. If you only buy and hold, staying on CoinSpot is often simpler. If you trade across many pairs, the hop to Binance can be worth the transaction/mining fee.

The direct PayID situation matters here too. With direct AUD deposits via BSB/PayID currently unavailable on Binance Australia, transferring crypto from another exchange such as CoinSpot is one available way to move assets into a Binance account. Frequently asked questions

Does CoinSpot charge to transfer to Binance?

Yes. CoinSpot says crypto sent to an external wallet is subject to a standard transaction (mining) fee. The current fee depends on the asset and network, and is shown in your CoinSpot wallet before you confirm the transfer. Binance does not charge a fee to receive a crypto deposit.

Do I have to use the same network on both sides?

Yes — this is the single most important rule. The deposit address you copy on Binance is tied to a specific network, and you must select that same network on CoinSpot. Sending on a different network can mean the coins don't arrive, or arrive on the wrong network and become hard to recover.

Can I send any coin from CoinSpot to Binance?

You can send any coin CoinSpot supports, as long as Binance also accepts deposits for that coin on a network that matches. Check that Binance lists the coin and the specific network before you start.

Do I need a destination tag or memo?

Only for certain assets. Some require a destination tag, memo or payment ID in addition to the address. If Binance displays one when you copy the address, you must enter it on CoinSpot too, or the deposit may not be credited to you.

How long does CoinSpot to Binance take?

It varies by network and by how many confirmations Binance requires. On fast networks it's often a few minutes; on a congested chain it can take longer. Track progress in Binance's deposit history.

What happens if I send on the wrong network?

Recovery depends on the asset, network and whether the receiving platform supports a recovery process. Some transfers may be recoverable, often with additional checks or fees, while others may be permanently lost. Sending a small test amount first is the safest way to avoid it.
