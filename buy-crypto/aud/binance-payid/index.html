Binance Australia PayID: Is AUD Deposit Back in 2026?

Public sources checked: 21 September 2026 (UTC) · By James Walker · Editor, OzCoinTrail

Binance's public AUD withdrawal FAQ still says bank-transfer deposits via BSB / PayID are temporarily unavailable. That confirms the wording on the help page; it does not establish what every Australian account can currently access. Before sending AUD, check whether your own Binance account offers the deposit method and provides current payment instructions.

This guide explains how to check availability, how direct AUD deposits differ from PayID payments on P2P, and what to compare if you consider funding Binance through another exchange.

Verification scope

This review covers public help pages and fee schedules. We have not tested an AUD deposit through an Australian Binance account.

What Binance's public help page actually confirms

The Binance AUD withdrawal FAQ, marked as updated on 2 April 2026, still carries an AUD bank-transfer deposit suspension notice. It also says withdrawals through this channel require a previous deposit.

A page's update date is not necessarily the date a service stopped or restarted. This FAQ alone does not establish a complete timeline of availability, or prove that every account has the same options.

For a payment you are about to make, check the current instructions in your account. If those instructions conflict with the help page, ask Binance support to clarify before sending funds.

How to check PayID availability in your account

  1. Open Binance directly. Use the official app or website and sign in.
  2. Look for the fiat deposit or Add Funds section and select AUD, if offered. Menu labels may differ between the app and website.
  3. Check for BSB / PayID or bank transfer. Look for any additional verification requirements, restrictions or maintenance notices.
  4. Read the current deposit instructions. Check the recipient details, payment reference, fees, minimum amount, limits and any bank-account name requirements.
  5. Do not reuse old payment details. Only proceed with instructions currently provided for your account. If the option is missing or suspended, confirm the position with official support.

Keep a record of the instructions and any transfer confirmation. A visible menu option alone is not proof that a payment has been accepted or credited.

Direct PayID deposits and P2P PayID payments are different

A direct AUD deposit follows Binance's fiat funding instructions. A P2P purchase is an order to buy crypto from another user, with payment made using the order's agreed method.

If your P2P marketplace shows an AUD offer accepting PayID, that does not establish that direct AUD deposits are available. Check the offer's price, limits, payment instructions and seller details before considering it. Keep communication and order handling on the platform.

Example: buying on CoinSpot and transferring to Binance

CoinSpot's official fee schedule lists free PayID and Direct Deposit funding, a 0.1% Markets trading fee, and a 1% Instant Buy, Sell & Swap fee. Check whether your chosen asset has a suitable Markets pair before assuming the lower fee applies.

For a hypothetical A$1,000 trade value, a 0.1% trading fee is A$1; a 1% fee is A$10. This compares the stated trading fees only. It does not compare execution prices or include withdrawal costs.

CoinSpot also lists a flat crypto withdrawal fee, with the current amount shown on its wallet page. Check that amount for the asset and network you intend to use.

Separate the costs into two stages:

  • Getting crypto to Binance: any AUD funding charge, the purchase fee, the effect of the execution price or spread, and the sending exchange's withdrawal fee. Check any receiving conditions or charges as well.
  • Trading after arrival: an additional trading fee or conversion cost if you choose to exchange the asset again.

Binance's published standard spot rate for regular users is 0.10% maker and taker, with a displayed 25% BNB discount for eligible fee payments. Pair-specific rates, promotions and account tiers can differ. This is a trading charge, not a fee you automatically pay merely because a transfer arrives.

Before transferring crypto: a practical checklist

  1. Check both accounts before buying. Complete any required verification and confirm you can withdraw from the sending platform and deposit into Binance.
  2. Match the exact asset and network. Both platforms must support the same network for that asset, with the relevant services enabled.
  3. Compare the purchase quote and withdrawal fee. A low trading fee does not guarantee the lowest overall cost.
  4. Copy the destination address from Binance's current deposit page. Include a memo or tag if that asset requires one.
  5. Check minimums and the net amount received. Allow for any withdrawal fee deducted from the amount sent.
  6. Consider a small test transfer. It must meet both platforms' minimums. A test and a second transfer may incur two withdrawal fees.
  7. Wait for the test to be credited before sending the rest. Check the transaction status and retain the transaction ID.

If you already hold the asset you want, compare whether a further trade is necessary. If you buy a different asset solely to transfer it and then swap it again, include both trades in your cost comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Is Binance PayID working in Australia right now?

This public-source review cannot confirm availability for your account. The AUD withdrawal FAQ still carries a bank-transfer deposit suspension notice. Check the current AUD deposit options and instructions after signing in.

Why might a news report and a help page disagree?

They may refer to different dates, service scopes or stages of availability. Without matching evidence, do not assume a page update proves a new suspension or that an announcement guarantees access for every account.

Can I use PayID on Binance P2P?

Only if your account can access a suitable offer that accepts it. A PayID payment for a P2P order is separate from direct AUD funding.

Is transferring through CoinSpot always cheaper than using a card?

No. Compare the purchase quote, trading and withdrawal fees, and any later conversion against the card's final quote and issuer charges. Fixed withdrawal fees can be significant for a small transfer.

Do I pay Binance's 0.10% spot fee just for receiving crypto?

No. The spot fee applies to a spot trade. Count it separately if you trade after the deposit arrives, using the rate applicable to your account and pair.

What should I do if AUD deposits are missing?

Check any account notices and contact official support. Do not send AUD using saved payment details from an earlier transaction. If considering another funding route, check its availability and full quote first.
