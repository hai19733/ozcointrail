How to Buy USDT in Australia

Last verified: 18 September 2026 · By OzCoinTrail Editorial Team

Buying USDT in Australia is easy — buying it cheaply is where the differences show up. If you're starting with AUD in an Australian bank account, CoinSpot Markets is one of the simplest low-fee routes: PayID deposits are free and the USDT/AUD market charges a 0.1% trading fee. Binance also charges a standard 0.10% spot trading fee, but its AUD bank-transfer deposits via BSB/PayID are currently unavailable, so your total cost depends on how you fund the account.

Two fees are especially easy to misunderstand: OKX Australia charges regular retail users 0.70% on standard trading pairs, while CoinSpot's Instant Buy, Sell & Swap service charges 1%. The key is to compare the full AUD → USDT route, not just an exchange's headline spot-trading fee.

What "buying USDT" actually means

USDT (Tether) is a stablecoin designed to track the US dollar. When you buy USDT with AUD, you're converting Australian dollars into a USD-pegged asset, so the price you pay moves with the AUD/USD exchange rate — roughly A$1.40 per USDT at the time of writing. People buy USDT to trade without moving back to AUD, to park funds between crypto trades, or to move money to another exchange without going through a bank.

For most Australian investors, USDT is treated as a CGT asset. Buying and holding it generally does not create a CGT event by itself, but selling, swapping or otherwise disposing of it can trigger a capital gain or loss. Different rules may apply if you're carrying on a crypto trading business.

The cheapest route depends on how your AUD gets in

The fee you pay to buy USDT is only half the cost. The other half is how AUD gets into the exchange in the first place — and that's where Binance and CoinSpot sit in opposite situations.

Starting from AUD → CoinSpot Markets (0.1%)

CoinSpot takes free AUD deposits via PayID or Direct Deposit, and its Markets order book charges a 0.1% trading fee. That makes the explicit platform fee for an AUD → USDT Markets trade 0.1%, with no PayID deposit fee on top. Your actual execution cost can still vary slightly with the order-book price and available liquidity.

Want the deepest liquidity and the lowest fee → Binance spot (0.10%)

Binance's standard spot fee is 0.10% (before any BNB discount), and its order books are among the deepest in crypto, which usually means a tighter price. The catch is the AUD on-ramp: Binance's official help centre currently lists AUD deposits via bank transfer (BSB/PayID) as temporarily unavailable, so you'd fund the account by card or through P2P instead — and both carry their own cost. For the full picture on Binance's AUD situation and its pending AFSL application, see our Binance Australia review.

The two traps to avoid

CoinSpot's default buy button is Instant Buy at 1%, not the 0.1% Markets fee — so switch to Markets before you trade. And OKX, which many round-ups call the cheap option, applies a 0.70% fee to standard trading pairs, including USDT/AUD. We break CoinSpot and Binance down head to head — including what CoinSpot's AFSL actually covers — in our Binance vs CoinSpot comparison.

The two numbers most guides get wrong

OKX's USDT/AUD fee is 0.70%, not 0.08%

OKX Australia's regular retail fee is currently 0.70% maker / 0.70% taker for standard pairs, excluding designated zero-fee pairs — and USDT/AUD sits in that standard-pair group. The often-quoted 0.08% / 0.10% rates come from different fee categories (such as wholesale or non-AUD tiers) and should not be applied automatically to a regular Australian retail account. That makes buying USDT with AUD on OKX materially more expensive than Binance or CoinSpot Markets, even though "OKX 0.08%" is what most guides quote. OKX still has a place for Australian traders — its licensed derivatives business, for one — but buying USDT with AUD isn't where it's cheap. See our OKX Australia review for where it does and doesn't make sense.

Binance PayID hasn't stayed restored

Some guides still say Binance "restored PayID in January 2026". That was reported at the time, but Binance's current help centre lists AUD deposits via bank transfer (BSB/PayID) as temporarily unavailable. Rather than relying on an old headline, check the deposit screen in your own account — that's the only place that reflects what's actually available to you right now.

Step by step

The steps below assume the cheapest common path — CoinSpot Markets — with notes for Binance where the two differ.

  1. Choose an AUSTRAC-registered exchange and create an account. If you want to compare the wider field before committing, start with our best crypto exchanges in Australia guide.
  2. Verify your identity. Australian exchanges require KYC — usually a government ID, and sometimes proof of address — before you can deposit or trade.
  3. Deposit AUD. On CoinSpot, use PayID or Direct Deposit (free). On Binance, while bank transfer is unavailable, use the card or P2P options shown in your account and check the fee before you confirm.
  4. Go to the order book, not the instant-buy button. On CoinSpot choose Markets; on Binance use the Spot tab.
  5. Buy USDT/AUD. Enter the AUD amount (or USDT amount) and place the order — a limit order lets you set your price, while a market order fills at the current price.
  6. Optional: withdraw USDT. If you're moving it to another exchange or wallet, pick a network both sides support — TRC-20 and ERC-20 are the most common — and check the network fee first. For a step-by-step on moving USDT to Binance specifically, see our CoinSpot to Binance transfer guide.

Frequently asked questions

What's the cheapest way to buy USDT in Australia?

For most people it's CoinSpot Markets at 0.1% with a free PayID deposit, or Binance spot at 0.10% if you can fund the account cheaply. Avoid CoinSpot's Instant Buy (1%) and OKX's USDT/AUD pair (0.70%).

Is OKX the cheapest place to buy USDT?

No. OKX's 0.08% rate applies to non-AUD pairs; USDT/AUD is an AUD-quoted pair and costs 0.70% for regular retail users. OKX is worth considering for other things, but not for buying USDT with AUD.

Can I deposit AUD into Binance with PayID?

Currently no — Binance's help centre lists AUD deposits via bank transfer (BSB/PayID) as temporarily unavailable. Check the deposit screen in your account, and use card or P2P in the meantime.

Why is Instant Buy more expensive than the order book?

Instant Buy bundles a spread into the price and adds a higher fee (1% on CoinSpot), instead of matching you with other traders at a 0.1% fee on the order book. On larger amounts the difference is significant.

Which network should I use to withdraw USDT?

There isn't one network that's always cheapest. TRC-20 is often a lower-cost option on exchanges that support it, while ERC-20 is widely supported but can cost more. Always compare the withdrawal fee shown by the sending exchange and make sure the receiving platform supports exactly the same network.

Is buying USDT taxable in Australia?

USDT is a CGT asset, so selling it can trigger a capital gain or loss. The gain from a stablecoin is usually small unless the AUD/USD exchange rate has moved a lot while you held it.
