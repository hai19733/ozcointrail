How to Buy Bitcoin in Australia

Last verified: 9 October 2026 · By James Walker · Editor, OzCoinTrail

Want to buy Bitcoin in Australia without paying unnecessary fees? The straightforward route is to choose a cryptocurrency exchange whose services are legally available in Australia, complete identity verification, deposit Australian dollars (AUD), and buy BTC through a spot market. Before opening an account, check the provider's AUSTRAC registration status, supported payment methods and fees.

For beginners, CoinSpot Markets charges 0.10% and supports free PayID deposits. Binance also offers a standard 0.10% spot trading fee, but its direct AUD bank deposit service is currently unavailable. That difference matters: a low trading fee does not guarantee a low total purchase cost.

If you're buying A$1,000 worth of Bitcoin, CoinSpot Markets' published 0.10% trading fee works out to A$1, compared with A$10 using CoinSpot Instant Buy at 1%, before any differences in execution price.

Quick answer: how to buy Bitcoin in Australia

  1. Choose an exchange that accepts Australian customers. To compare the wider field first, see our guide to the best crypto exchanges in Australia.
  2. Register and complete identity verification (KYC).
  3. Deposit AUD using an available payment method, preferably fee-free PayID where supported.
  4. Open the Markets or Spot trading screen.
  5. Buy BTC and check your wallet balance.

You do not need to buy one whole Bitcoin. You can purchase a fraction with a relatively small amount of money, subject to the exchange's minimum order.

What buying Bitcoin in Australia actually involves

Buying Bitcoin means exchanging Australian dollars or another supported asset for BTC through a cryptocurrency trading service.

Bitcoin is legal to buy and hold in Australia. Crypto service providers operating in Australia are subject to applicable financial-crime regulations, including AUSTRAC registration requirements.

Before making your first purchase, understand three separate steps:

  • Funding: getting AUD or another supported asset onto the exchange.
  • Trading: converting that balance into Bitcoin.
  • Storage: keeping Bitcoin on the exchange or withdrawing it to a personal wallet.

These steps can involve different charges. A platform advertising a 0.10% trading fee may still be relatively expensive if the payment method charges an additional fee or the quoted Bitcoin price is unfavourable.

The key decision is not simply which exchange has the lowest trading fee. It is which available route gives you the most BTC for your AUD after all charges.

How much does it cost to buy A$1,000 of Bitcoin?

The trading fee is the easiest cost to compare, but it is not always the largest. A Bitcoin purchase can involve four costs:

  1. Deposit or payment processing fees.
  2. Trading commissions.
  3. Spread or execution-price differences.
  4. Withdrawal fees if you transfer BTC to another wallet.

Trading fee vs spread: what is the difference?

A trading fee is a commission charged for executing the transaction. A spread is the difference between the buy and sell prices quoted in a market or purchase service, and the price you actually receive may also be affected by slippage.

Suppose one exchange quotes Bitcoin at A$150,000 while another quotes A$151,500. The second quote is 1% higher before any additional transaction charges. That price difference can matter more than a fraction-of-a-percent difference in advertised fees.

Before confirming a purchase, compare the final BTC amount you will receive for the same AUD budget.

How to buy Bitcoin with PayID in Australia

PayID is an Australian payment method that lets you send money using a registered identifier such as an email address or phone number instead of entering traditional bank-account details. It is commonly used to fund crypto exchange accounts. CoinSpot publishes a zero-fee PayID deposit option, although banks can delay or restrict transfers.

Step 1: Create and verify your account

Open your chosen exchange using its official website or application, register using your email, and complete the identity verification process. You may be asked to provide your legal name and date of birth, an Australian residential address, a driver's licence or passport, and a selfie or additional identity evidence. Enable two-factor authentication before depositing funds.

Step 2: Find the AUD deposit screen

On CoinSpot, open the account funding section, select AUD, and choose PayID if it is available. The exchange will display the payment details needed to fund your account. Do not send money to a PayID copied from a social media message or a person claiming to represent customer support.

Step 3: Transfer AUD from your bank

Open your banking application and enter the deposit instructions supplied by the exchange, checking the recipient and any required payment reference. Send a small first transfer if you want to confirm the payment details are correct. A PayID payment may be processed quickly, but first-time transfers, bank checks and payment restrictions can cause delays.

Step 4: Wait for your AUD balance

Once the deposit is credited, confirm that the AUD balance appears in your exchange account. Do not attempt a second payment simply because the first one is delayed — check the bank transaction status and exchange deposit history first.

Step 5: Buy BTC through Markets

On CoinSpot, open Markets, locate the available BTC/AUD market and review the order screen. The important distinction is that CoinSpot's standard Instant Buy screen is not the same as Markets. After execution, your Bitcoin balance should appear in your account.

Before submitting your order, make sure you are using CoinSpot's Markets trading interface, which has a published 0.10% fee. CoinSpot separately lists Take Profit, Stop and Limit Orders at 1%. Check the specific order type and fee shown before confirming your purchase.

For beginners who want to minimise explicit trading fees while funding directly in AUD, the combination to look for is fee-free PayID deposits plus a 0.10% Markets trade.

How to buy Bitcoin on Binance in Australia

Binance is an alternative for eligible Australian users, particularly those who already hold cryptocurrency or can fund their account using an available supported method. Its standard spot trading fee is commonly 0.10%, but direct AUD funding remains the important complication.

Step 1: Create and verify your Binance account

Open Binance through its official website or app, create an account, and complete the identity verification required for your region and selected products. Confirm that the account is eligible to use the relevant trading services.

Step 2: Check available payment options

Before trying to buy BTC, go to Binance's funding or Add Funds section. Binance's Australian support materials currently state that direct AUD bank deposits through BSB/PayID are temporarily unavailable. Depending on your account, payment routes shown may include supported debit or credit card purchases, third-party payment providers, peer-to-peer trading where available, or cryptocurrency transfers from another exchange or wallet. Availability and fees must be checked in the account.

Do not assume you can transfer AUD from your bank to Binance for free just because Binance advertises a low spot trading fee. For the current state of play, see our guide on Binance Australia PayID deposits.

Step 3: Fund the account

Choose a payment method that is actually available to you. For a card purchase, review the full quote and processing charge. For a crypto transfer, confirm the supported asset and blockchain network — sending funds using an incompatible network can cause a loss of funds. For P2P transactions, remain within the platform's supported process and check counterparties carefully.

Step 4: Open Spot trading

When your Binance account has an eligible trading balance, open the Spot trading screen and select a BTC trading pair supported for your account and available balance. For example, someone already holding USDT may be able to use a BTC/USDT spot pair — that is different from buying BTC directly with AUD.

Step 5: Review and execute the order

You may encounter two common order types: a market order executes against available orders at current market prices, while a limit order lets you set the price at which you are willing to buy but may not execute. Check the trading fee, quantity, order price and estimated Bitcoin received, and confirm only when the details match your intention.

Is Binance cheaper than CoinSpot?

On the standard spot trading commission alone, both are listed at 0.10%. But there is a major practical difference: CoinSpot supports free AUD PayID deposits, while Binance currently does not offer the same straightforward BSB/PayID funding route. If using Binance requires a card fee, a P2P premium or an additional conversion, those charges can make the total purchase more expensive.

Binance is worth considering when you can fund it efficiently. CoinSpot Markets is generally more straightforward for someone starting with AUD in an Australian bank. For the deeper comparison, see our Binance vs CoinSpot guide.

Why your bank may delay a crypto payment

Even when an exchange accepts PayID, your bank may apply its own fraud-prevention checks, including delays on first-time payments, limits on transfers to cryptocurrency businesses, additional fraud verification, or blocks on certain recipients. The exchange cannot guarantee that your bank will release a payment immediately. If an AUD deposit is delayed, check both the bank's transfer record and your exchange's deposit instructions before taking further action.

Can you buy Bitcoin with A$50 or A$100?

Yes. Bitcoin can be divided into very small fractions, and exchanges generally allow purchases smaller than one BTC. The exact minimum depends on the exchange, trading pair and payment method. You do not need to purchase one whole Bitcoin.

For example, if Bitcoin were trading at A$150,000, a A$100 purchase would represent approximately 0.00066667 BTC before fees. This is an arithmetic illustration, not a live BTC price quote. For a small first purchase, pay special attention to fixed payment charges and minimum order sizes — a fixed charge can represent a larger percentage of a small transaction.

The amount you can afford to lose matters more than buying a particular fraction of one Bitcoin.

What should you do after buying Bitcoin?

Once your order has executed, you will usually see BTC in the exchange's wallet or account balance. You then need to decide whether to leave it on the exchange or transfer it to a wallet you control.

Keeping Bitcoin on an exchange

An exchange account is convenient for buying, selling and reviewing transaction history. However, you depend on the platform's custody, account-security and withdrawal arrangements. Turn on two-factor authentication, use a unique password and review account security settings.

Moving Bitcoin to your own wallet

A self-custody wallet lets you control the private keys to your Bitcoin. It reduces dependence on exchange custody, but introduces different risks: if you lose the recovery phrase or send BTC to an incompatible destination, recovering funds may be impossible. For a first withdrawal, verify the destination address and use a small test transfer when practical, and review the network fee before confirming. Australian Binance users should also be aware that additional information requirements for crypto transfers began rolling out from 1 July 2026.

Is buying Bitcoin legal in Australia?

Yes. Buying, owning and selling Bitcoin is generally legal in Australia. Australia regulates relevant cryptocurrency service providers under financial-crime laws, and AUSTRAC maintains a public register of virtual asset service providers that you can use to verify a business's registration status. However, AUSTRAC registration is not an investment guarantee, and it does not mean a cryptocurrency's price is protected — Bitcoin remains a volatile asset and can lose substantial value.

Do you pay tax when buying Bitcoin in Australia?

Purchasing Bitcoin directly with Australian dollars generally does not, by itself, trigger a capital gains tax event. The purchase establishes information relevant to the Bitcoin's cost base. A later disposal may create a tax obligation — including when you sell BTC for AUD, exchange BTC for another cryptocurrency, or spend BTC on goods or services in circumstances where CGT applies.

Your tax treatment depends on your circumstances, including whether you are investing or carrying on a business. Keep records of purchase dates, AUD values, trading charges, disposal proceeds and relevant wallet transfers. Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to buy Bitcoin in Australia?

CoinSpot Markets is one of the simplest low-fee options for Australians funding from a local bank account: its published market trading fee is 0.10% and PayID deposits are free. Binance Spot also has a standard 0.10% fee, but you must account for its available funding methods and any additional charges.

Can I buy Bitcoin with PayID?

Yes. Australian exchanges such as CoinSpot support PayID deposits. Once your AUD deposit arrives, you can use the available trading interface to buy BTC. Your bank may impose transfer limits or temporary holds.

Can I buy Bitcoin on Binance with AUD?

Binance offers payment routes for eligible Australian users, but direct AUD bank deposits through BSB/PayID are currently listed as unavailable. Check the payment methods shown in your account and compare the complete quoted cost.

Is Binance or CoinSpot cheaper for buying Bitcoin?

On the trading fee alone they're the same at 0.10%. In practice CoinSpot is usually the cheaper all-in route for someone starting with AUD, because its PayID deposits are free while Binance's AUD bank deposits are currently unavailable.

Is OKX cheaper than Binance in Australia?

Not necessarily. Under OKX Australia's 2026 retail fee framework, the regular rate is 0.70% maker and taker for standard fee-group pairs, compared with Binance's typical 0.10% spot trading fee. Actual costs depend on account eligibility, trading pair and funding method.

Can I buy A$100 worth of Bitcoin?

Usually, yes. Bitcoin is divisible and exchanges permit fractional purchases, subject to minimum trade sizes. Check the minimum order and any payment charges before confirming.

Do I need a Bitcoin wallet before buying?

No. Most custodial exchanges provide an account wallet for purchased BTC. A separate self-custody wallet is optional and becomes relevant when you want direct control over your Bitcoin's private keys.

Do I pay tax immediately when buying Bitcoin?

Buying BTC directly with AUD generally does not itself create a capital gains tax event. Tax consequences may arise later when you sell, swap or otherwise dispose of the Bitcoin. Keep accurate transaction records.

The bottom line: how should you buy Bitcoin in Australia?

If you have Australian dollars in a bank account and want to purchase Bitcoin with relatively low explicit fees, start by comparing CoinSpot Markets and its free PayID deposit option. If you already use Binance, or have an inexpensive supported way to fund it, its 0.10% standard spot trading fee makes it worth evaluating.

Avoid choosing an exchange from the advertised trading percentage alone. Compare the funding charges, final BTC quote, withdrawal fees and available services before making the purchase. Buying Bitcoin should be a deliberate financial decision, not a race to complete the transaction.
